Appearance UX · decision sheet

T2.S8 U: how a creator picks, applies, edits, inspects, repairs and reuses colours and materials, built on the merged S8 engine (A–G, E7) and the Light & Sky language. Design study for owner review. Not approved, not a production contract, and nothing here is imported by product code. The prototype simulates S8's operations; the thumbnail lab approximates its renderer.

PrototypeThe recommended instrument end to end: Paint, tray, Library, Finish Card, inspector, import, repair, transfer. ConceptsCompeting directions for six questions, with the recommended and rejected answers side by side. Thumbnail labAll 21 catalogue entries in six presentations at real sizes, measured with E7's distinctness method. Recommendation one instrument for colours and materials

  • Paint is one dock key. Arming it opens a small tray above the dock with two tabs, Colours and Materials: favourites, then recents, then Any colour (wheel) or Browse all (Library). Keys 1–9 pick, T switches tab, S toggles Face / Space, I or Alt-click samples.
  • Shape tells the consequence. A circle is a colour: it recolours the surface and keeps its relief and sheen (walnut stays grained). A rounded square is a material: it replaces the whole Finish. One grammar in the tray, Library, Card and Index.
  • The Library is a sheet, not a mode. Search, sources (Curated, This project, My library, later third parties), filters and categories over 128 px sample cards. One click chooses and closes; expanding a card shows the detail without leaving the grid.
  • Editing is contextual. Clicking a surface opens its Subject Card; a Finish row opens the Finish Card. Reach is chosen before the edit (This part · All n), and gold outlines show who it reaches.
  • Progressive Finish Card. Colour (Natural / Tint / Colour), Sheen as named detents with exact values on demand, Pattern (scale, direction), Relief; channels, mapping and diagnostics fold into accordions.
  • Inspection never writes. The inspector compares shape, light and channel, and A/B against the curated base; changing anything there changes the view, not the project.
  • Professional work stays in the same Card. Import is a sheet with blocking collisions and a normal-direction check. Repair is an amber strip on the affected surfaces and the Index. Save to library, then insert in another project; the copy is independent.
  • Thumbnails are flat lit sample tiles framed on a physical window, one stored 256 px master per Finish. See U1.

The complete flow each step opens the prototype at that state

  1. Arm Paint

    Paint key or B. The tray appears on the dock with the last tab used.

    open ↗
  2. Pick

    A tray swatch, a palette colour, the wheel, the dropper, or a Library card. A pick made elsewhere leads the tray row as picked, so what is armed is always visible where it is used.

    Library ↗ · colours ↗
  3. Apply

    Hover shows a cobalt ghost and a consequence line (Walnut Veneer replaces Painted Plaster on North wall). Click paints one face; drag paints several; Space or Shift-click paints the whole room or object. One undo per stroke.

    open ↗
  4. Edit in context

    Click the painted surface → Subject Card → Finish Card. Reach first, then Colour, Sheen, Pattern, Relief. Dragging previews (never history); release commits one undo step, coalesced for 600 ms.

    open ↗
  5. Inspect

    Sphere, block or plane; studio, grazing or the current room; colour, roughness, metal, normal or full; A/B against the curated base.

    open ↗
  6. Save and reuse

    Star to keep it in the tray; Save to library from the Card foot (no project change); in another project insert it from My library as an independent copy.

    open ↗

Ownership rules one writer per intent; everything else only chooses

Pickers, the tray, the Library, the Index and the inspector never write project state. They arm a pick or build a patch and hand it to exactly one writer. A drag preview is transient render state and never enters history. Favourites and recents are creator preferences, not project data.

Rules the design depends on

  • Reach is chosen before the edit, and defaults by kind. A curated Finish defaults to This part (editing walnut should not silently fork every walnut surface into a project Finish); a project Finish (fin:) defaults to All n and edits in place.
  • Materializing reaches one kind. All on a curated or model Finish reaches only same-kind users (F4 refuses a compound Scene + Layout registration); the Card says which users keep the original and how to share it with them afterwards. Editing an existing fin: still reaches Layout and Scene users together.
  • A pristine clone is the curated normal form. Resetting every override returns surfaces to cat:, as E's review follow-up does.
  • Sheen shows the effective value. Roughness and metalness are factors on the ORM map; the Card and inspector show factor × scanned mean with an As scanned detent, never the bare factor.
  • No duplicated writers. The Index lists Finishes and opens the Card; it does not edit them. The Library chooses; it does not edit. There is no second Finish list beside the Index.

Recommendation

  • Sample tile: flat, lit, with a soft sheen band across the top third so gloss and metal read, framed on a physical window (two or three boards, one brick course, a few linen threads), not a repeat count. It reads true to colour (mean colour shift ΔE00 4.7 against 46.5 for the E7 references at 192 px) with no clipping.
  • One stored master per Finish: 256 px PNG. XS (28 CSS, tighter window), S (56) and M (128, 2×) derive from it. The L detail (sphere, grazing close-up) and the inspector render on demand through the E7 host, one at a time, so opening a list never fetches full-resolution images.
  • Framing hint outside the recipe: window size and centre per catalogue entry, in the same side table as thumbnails and locators. Project and imported Finishes default to a window from their tile size.
  • New thumbnail recipe version; E7's inspection recipe 2 and its 192 px references stay intact as engineering references.

Owner decisions each with a recommendation

  1. Smooth and Polished concrete.

    No presentation separates them (same scan, factor only). Recommend one card, Smooth Concrete, with a Matte / Polished variant chip, and F's rule applied between cards, not between a card's variants. Alternative: drop Polished from Core. U cannot close while this is open.

  2. Reach before the edit, replacing the post-hoc Ask.

    The plan keeps PLATE's Ask semantics. The study asks the same question earlier: a This part · All n switch on the Card, gold outlines on who it reaches, defaults by kind. Nothing is asked after a drag. Recommend adopting it; it removes a dialog from every shared edit.

  3. Colour coat as new engineering.

    A colour applied with Paint keeps relief and sheen and becomes a deduplicated derived Finish (ten walls painted Sage make one Finish, following the palette). Recommend a dedicated planner over E4 primitives.

  4. G thumbnail slot to 256 px.

    Needed for a 2× M card. Recommend a scoped change: PNG, ≤ 256 KiB, edge 256, new thumbnail recipe key. Old 192 items stay valid and display with upscale until regenerated.

  5. Same-kind reach on materialize.

    All on a curated or model Finish reaches only same-kind users, with the Card naming who keeps the original. Recommend accepting as the E handoff's two-step journey; widening F4 is a separate decision.

  6. Sheen display.

    Show effective roughness (factor × scanned mean) with an As scanned detent; exact values labelled as factors. Recommend adopting.

  7. Tray A or mixed row B.

    Both are drawn in Concepts. Recommend A (tabs) for the clarity of consequence; B if the owner prefers fewer clicks over that clarity.

Limits of this study

  • Simulated operations and history; no file IO (imports use stand-in texture sets), no network, no real Camera.
  • The lab renderer is a WebGL2 approximation of the metallic–roughness path. Decisions on presentation and crop are portable; absolute numbers must be re-measured on the E7 host (U1).
  • No creator study. The four tasks were walked by the author, not by creators.
  • Coarse pointer designed but not checked on an iPad; the board's iframes cannot emulate one.
