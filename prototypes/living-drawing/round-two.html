Living Drawings · Round two · Expressiveness without complexity

Every surface is a place

No new tools. The same three gestures now land on more things: the top of something you made, the space along a wall, and one of many. What you meant is kept as a description, so it keeps holding while the room changes.

Round one let you draw on the room. Round two lets you draw on your own work and along it. A circle on a plinth’s top can be pushed into a hollow, then all the way through. A panel drawn once can be drawn along, around a corner, and the room divides it. One panel can differ from the rest, and it remembers why: “the middle one”, “centred under the niche”. When that stops being true, it doesn’t quietly jump to another panel. It stays where it was and asks.

The question

More you can say, nothing new to learn

The follow-up asked how Living Drawings could become significantly more expressive without becoming significantly harder to use. Specifically: drawing to add or remove form (a circle pushed into a through-hole), forms that aren’t symmetrical (an arm at one end of a bench, a pedestal whose front differs from its back), and repetition that understands its context (panels around a room, ornament along a curve).

The research added four things. Creators lose relationships. Duplicated details are hard to revise. Boolean libraries fail on ordinary cases, such as coplanar cuts. And attaching things by proximity can silently redirect them after a revision.

The question is not how many new shapes the language can produce. It is how much more freedom the same simple language can unlock.

Our answer has three parts, and none of them is a new command. Each lets an existing gesture land somewhere new, and each stores intent as a description rather than a position or a nearest neighbour. That one decision is what lets everything keep working while the room changes.

Every surface is a place

The top of a form and the seat of a bench are hosts, like the floor and the walls. One dial adds, carves, or goes through.

Repetition is drawing along

Draw a panel once, then draw along from it. The repetition is a property of the panel, and the architecture divides it.

Exceptions remember why

Draw over one of many and it changes them all. Then Only this one keeps the difference, described as “the middle one” or “under the niche”.

Discovery one

Every surface is a place

In round one you could draw on the floor and on the walls. Now any flat face of something you made is a place to draw too: the top of a plinth, the top of a pedestal, the seat of a bench. The pen tip says which: “Plinth’s top”, “Bench’s seat”. The gestures are exactly the same.

A loop drawn on a place gets the usual handle, which now works as a dial. Pull it up and the drawing becomes a form standing there (a riser, an arm). Push it in and it becomes a hollow. Keep pushing past the far side and it snaps to through: an opening. On a wall, through makes a window, or a doorway if it reaches the floor. It is one signed value, so there is no union-or-subtract choice to make first, and the drawing survives every state. Drag an opening back up through zero and the same loop stands on the top instead.

Asymmetry by composition

An arm at one end of a bench is not a special feature of benches. It is its own simple form, standing on the bench’s seat, and its sentence says exactly where:

Place · on Bench’s seatArmA form standing on Bench’s seat, at its right end, 22 cm tall. Its profile is straight.

“At its right end” is a description, re-found after every change. Widen the room and the bench’s end, tied to the right wall, moves 60 cm; the arm moves with it. Lower the seat in the bench’s section and the arm comes down 5 cm. Both are narrated. But some changes alter what “the seat” means: a seat that moved more than 15 cm at once, a seat redrawn as a slope, an arm that would now run around the bench’s corner, or a bench that is gone. Then the arm goes slack. It keeps its place, turns gold, and asks: follow the seat, keep it here, or remove it. It never jumps to whatever happens to be nearest.

A pedestal whose front differs from its back is the other kind of asymmetry. That is discovery three.

Discovery two

Repetition is drawing along

Draw a panel on the wall and pull it out 2 cm. Then start inside it and draw along: across the wall, around the corner. Ghost outlines show the division while the pen moves, and on release the panel becomes a rhythm. That is the whole gesture. There is no count to type and no spacing to compute, because the room already knows both.

The rhythm divides itself by the architecture. Each stretch holds whole panels. By default they flex to share the stretch evenly and the gap stays exact; tap sharing each stretch evenly to make them keep their drawn size instead and let the gaps grow. Stretches break at corners, at wall ends, and at any opening that crosses the band, so a window cut through the panelling makes the panels divide around it. When the room changes, the division is redone and narrated: “Panelling re-divided the back wall: 8 of 59 cm (was 7 of 61 cm).”

Repetition is a property of the form, not a new kind of object. It is a phrase in the panel’s sentence:

Rhythm · raisedPanellingA panel drawn on the back wall, standing out 2 cm, repeated along the back and right walls: 7 + 6, about 61 cm wide, 12 cm apart, sharing each stretch evenly. Its profile is your own drawing.

Switch the repetition off and the single panel is back where you drew it; switch it on and every exception returns too. Draw over any one panel and they all change, because they are one drawing. That is the answer to “duplicated details are hard to revise”: there are no duplicates to revise. The bevel drawn once in the profile lens is on every panel.

Why not an Array tool

An array stores a count and a spacing. Those are exactly the numbers the architecture should decide, so an array goes wrong as soon as the wall changes. It also turns one detail into many objects that then have to be edited one by one or regrouped. Drawing along stores only the intent (this panel, along these walls, about this far apart) and lets the room supply the rest.

Discovery three

Exceptions remember what they are about

Drawing over one of many changes all of them. The whisper then offers the exception: Only this one for a panel, Only the front for a pedestal. It is the proposed-tie pattern from round one: offered, never assumed.

An exception is stored as a description, never as an index or a position: “the middle one on the back wall”, “the 3rd from the left”, “centred under Niche”, “the front”. After every change, each description is found again. If it still holds, the exception follows and the note says how. If it no longer holds, the exception goes slack. It keeps its shape and its last place, is marked in gold, and asks what it should be. It never moves on to a different panel by itself.

A description can also hold while its effect changes. If the arched panel had been described as “the 3rd from the left” and the room widened, it would still be the 3rd from the left, and the note would say it no longer sits under the niche, with a Tie it there action. Sitting under something is never treated as a tie.

Only the front

For a mass, drawing over its side changes the shared profile all around. Only the front keeps that drawing as an offset on top of the shared profile, for the front only, blended into the sides (soft or crisp). Because it is stored as the strokes you drew rather than as a separate shape, later changes still flow through. Draw a taper over the back and the front keeps its bow, on top of the taper. Swap the sculpture for a wider one and the plan refits; the front, and a seat carved into the top, come along.

The ambitious scenario

Pushed further, outside the journey

  • A window cutting through the band. The rhythm makes way: the stretch splits around it and each side re-divides. A doorway on the right wall in the gallery does the same.
  • A stretch too short for a panel. If a stretch fits less than about half a panel, it stays empty. One panel in a slightly short stretch flexes, and on a slightly long one it stretches, by up to about a third in the cases we tried. The flex mode is honest about this; keeping their size leaves gaps instead.
  • Dentils under the cornice. A 6 cm block drawn near the ceiling and drawn along: 54 dentils that follow the room. Narrow the room and they re-divide like the panels.
  • Beads along the niche’s arch (the curve experiment). A small bead drawn beside the niche, then drawn along its edge. The rhythm follows the edge instead of the walls, and divides at the edge’s corners as it does at a room’s. The beads keep their size and their distance from the edge, turned to the tangent. For engineering

The simplest representation that holds

No general boolean

The research was right to be wary of mesh CSG. Coplanar faces, shared edges and near-degenerate slivers are exactly what drawn architecture produces: a hole whose top is flush with the plinth top, an opening whose side is the wall’s plane. Repairing those results after the fact doesn’t make them correct. So carving here is host-local. It happens only along the direction the host was built in, as part of building the host:

  • A hollow or opening in a mass’s top is an inner loop of the mass’s own sweep: a top cap with a hole, an inner wall, and a floor cap or none. Before building, the hole is checked against every ring of the profile. If it would break out of the side (a 40 cm hole in a waisted pedestal), it stops where it still fits. The note says so, and the dial reads “stops at 19 cm”.
  • Wall openings are holes in the wall’s own outline, with reveals 20 cm deep. A doorway touches the floor, so the outline is notched instead of pierced.
  • Runs carry, but don’t carve. An arm stands on a bench’s seat, but pushing a seat drawing down is refused, with the reason. Carving a run would mean cutting across a swept section; that is where a general boolean would start.

Invalid results aren’t repaired here. These cases simply can’t produce them, and the cost is a narrower boundary, which we name below.

References, not proximity

A hosted form’s host is a description: {form, face: top | seat, anchor}, such as “Plinth’s top, centred” or “Bench’s seat, at its right end”. It is re-resolved on every evaluation, in dependency order, and its last frame is cached. When the description stops being true, the form goes slack and uses the cache. The same rule covers exceptions (re-resolved descriptions plus the strokes you drew) and the rhythm itself (repeat: {along, gap, mode, exceptions} on an ordinary relief or recess).

One kernel, still

Everything is still the profiled sweep from round one. It now accepts one section per path station, which is how “only the front” blends into the sides. Rhythm instances are swept from the shared motif and merged into one mesh per form.

Performance

The finished gallery has about 120 instances. In the first build, evaluating it took 73 ms, because each bead re-measured its own shape. With per-motif measurement and a cache of creased normals (unchanged geometry reuses its normals), it takes about 7 ms. That includes dragging the room wider, when every rhythm re-divides.

Honest account

What building it taught us

Held

Descriptions beat positions

Every case that would have failed silently with nearest-neighbour attachment instead produced a sentence: the arm on a sloped seat, the middle of an even row, the panel under a niche that is gone. The slack state, built for round one’s frame, turned out to be the general answer.

Changed

A slack exception has to stay visible

At first, an exception whose description failed simply disappeared: with no middle panel, nothing was arched. Technically nothing was lost, but it looked lost. It now keeps its shape at its last place, the rest of the wall divides around it, and it is dashed in gold until the creator decides. One caveat: “its last place” depends on where the description last held during a drag, not just on the final room.

Held

Composition covers most asymmetry

The arm and the riser are ordinary forms, so they get profiles, materials, oversketch and ties for free. Neither benches nor masses needed a special feature.

Changed

The dial needed a refusal

Pulling and pushing one handle through zero reads well on a top. On a bench’s seat, pushing down has no valid result. Silently clamping felt broken, so it now whispers why: a seat can carry things but can’t be carved.

Broke

Curves bend motifs

Beads keep their size and turn with the edge. Around the arch of a niche that works for small motifs, but a panel-sized motif on the same curve would overlap on the inside of the curve and gap on the outside. Fixing that means bending the motif: deformation, which drawing doesn’t express. The prototype keeps motifs rigid and leaves this boundary visible.

Broke

Exceptions are stored around the motif’s centre

Exception strokes are kept relative to the motif’s centre. Reshape the motif so that its centre moves a lot, and an exception can drift with it. It is a representation detail with a visible effect; anchoring exception strokes to the motif’s outline instead would fix it.

Changed

Tidy can erase a deliberate difference

A 4 cm taper over 85 cm was straightened into a vertical line by Tidy’s axis snapping, which removed exactly the difference between front and back. The journey now draws a curved taper. Snapping should probably stay off when drawing over an existing silhouette; that needs testing with real hands.

Changed

Many instances need cheap evaluation

Round one rebuilt everything on every change, and that was fine at twelve forms. At a hundred and twenty instances it wasn’t (73 ms). Measuring each motif once and caching normals for unchanged geometry fixed it without making anything incremental or stateful.

The boundary

Where complexity stops paying

Each row is something we could have built. The verdict weighs what it would add against what a creator would have to understand, and against how reliably it could work.

Remaining limits

What the prototype doesn’t do yet

  • Places exist on the flat tops of masses and on the seats of floor runs. The faces of reliefs, the sides of masses and runs on walls are not places yet.
  • A mass can have one side exception, and it is offered only for masses standing on the floor.
  • Rhythms run along the walls or along a niche’s edge. Ceiling and floor rhythms, and rhythms of masses on the floor (a row of plinths), are not built.
  • Draw-along has to start inside the motif. On an iPad the motif must be on screen when the stroke starts.
  • Exception strokes are stored relative to the motif’s centre (see the findings).
  • A doorway’s threshold is just the floor; door leaves and frames would be a frame (round one) following its edge.
