B. Proposed reconciliation
I recommend five substantive refinements:
1. Recognize component-centered experiences explicitly. Inspection, supported decomposition, annotation, and reassembly fit the existing category. They should not require a Room or a traversable camera journey.
2. Preserve supported component meaning through ingest. Protect hierarchy, local transforms, origins, materials, clips, and addressable identity where available and supported. Do not equate preservation with mechanical understanding.
3. Separate inspection from presentation. Temporary opening/separation is session state. Saved choreography is authored behavior. Evaluated transforms are derived playback state.
4. Clarify direction ownership. Camera owns camera direction; Experience owns visitor-facing bindings. Presentation motion needs a deliberate owner when planned. A shared clock may coordinate these without absorbing them into Camera.
5. Make revision survival explicit. Preserve unrelated work; update relationships only under an authored policy; expose missing or ambiguous references instead of silently retargeting them.
The commonality with P26 should initially be an interaction and ownership principle: focus on something, expose it reversibly, retain its identity, and recover context. A curved Wall’s station-based unfolding and a watch component’s parent-relative transform are different mechanisms. There is insufficient evidence to prescribe one implementation abstraction for both.
I would keep prototype governance, experiment thresholds, competitor inventories, pricing, and detailed playback algorithms outside the northstar. They belong in operational policy or scoped plans. I would also defer adding video export as a signature promise: it is not necessary to reconcile component authoring.
