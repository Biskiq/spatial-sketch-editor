12Environment: a sky, a horizon and light, Paper by default

Preset
Seen in
EnvironmentWorld · saved

Sky, horizon and light. Rooms, floors and your own lights stay yours.

While editing, the Stage shows

Picking a preset is one change with one Undo. Preview, snapshots and the published visit always use it.
Scope: a decent, bounded round (R5), about the size of one Card redesign. The look is a fixed table of presets. The work is one shared environment component, one saved Scene field and one picker. Nothing in Layout changes, so rooms and floors stay editable on their own.

In R5

  • Four presets: Paper (default, today's look), Daylight, Golden hour, Dusk. Each fixes a sky gradient, ground colour and horizon haze, hemisphere fill, one sun (direction, colour, soft shadow) and exposure.
  • One runtime-safe preset table and one environment component, shared by the Stage, snapshots, Preview and the published visit. No editor module reaches the visitor chunk.
  • Scene saves environment: { preset } as World presentation. The Scene format number goes up, older files are refused, and fixtures are regenerated.
  • The picker sits in the World Inspector when nothing is selected. "Plain Paper while editing" is a Stage preference and isn't saved.

Later, not now

  • Custom colours, sun position and time-of-day sliders
  • Image-based lighting (HDRI), clouds, weather, an animated sky
  • Changing the environment per Stop or during a tour (Experience)
  • Fog and render quality as their own controls

Cost and risk

  • The sky is one gradient dome and the ground one disc that fades into the haze: two draw calls.
  • The sun is the only new shadow caster. Today only authored lights cast shadows on the Stage. The sun joins the existing on-demand shadow invalidation, so it re-renders on change, not every frame. Its shadow camera fits the Layout bounds.
  • Authored lights add on top. Dusk is dim on purpose, so a room's own lamps carry it.
  • Paper stays the default. A new World looks exactly as it does now, and Preview shows the same Paper without the grid (never black).
  • On the Stage an environment keeps Paper's bounded sheet and its grid; the sky, the ground around the sheet and the light change, and the sun's shadows land on the sheet. Grid on or off applies as on Paper. Visitors and snapshots never see the grid.
  • Presets are stylized, not photographic: flat gradients, soft shadows, warm paper-like tones, the same art direction as the shell.

What should a visitor see first?

  • A ready draft. The Experience lens is ready the moment you enter it: an unnamed draft and the start card. Nothing is saved yet, so looking around never dirties the project or adds an Undo step. The first real step (Capture, Present an object, Place a View) creates the Experience and its first Presentation as one change, and one Undo takes both back.
  • An Experiences group at the top of the Index, the way a design file lists its pages. It uses the Index's own language: the small uppercase group title with a count, 28 px sans rows, gold tint on the current one, ••• on hover for Rename, Duplicate and Delete. The + is a quiet ink icon at the end of the group title, no ring or fill, the same treatment every Index group can use later. One click switches, and there is no menu to open. 15A Biskiq cursor set

One family: a forest-ink shape with a white rim, readable on Paper, on the dark mat and on every environment. A gold badge means "this adds something". Hover each tile to try its cursor.

Hover the Stage: empty Paper, the object, the selected View's dot and frustum, the path.

  • Selection is the arrow: on empty Stage, on objects, on Views, on buttons and in the Index. Things you can click to select never turn the cursor into a hand.
  • The hand means grabbing: the open hand only while Space is held, or over something you drag as a whole, and the closed hand only while a drag is under way (pan, orbit, a dragged point).
  • Each gesture says what it will do: turn over a selected frustum, move over a selected View's dot, add point over a selected path, place View while Place a View is armed, look while looking through, and not allowed where a press would be refused.
  • A gesture hint is a small hint tag beside the cursor, never a toast. It hides after the first use in a session.
  • Build: one cursors.css with --cursor-* properties, each url(data:image/svg+xml,…) x y, <system fallback>, at 32 px with a 2× image-set. The Stage's data-cursor uses these names.

16Turn a View by its frustum

  • Press anywhere in the selected View's frustum and drag: the View turns about its eye. Height, distance and lens stay. A dashed arc shows the turn, with the angle.
  • The dot moves the View. Shift snaps the turn to 15°. Release is one change and one Undo, and nothing is said.
  • In 3D, the selected camera's frustum lines turn it the same way, on the floor.

17Present this places a camera you can see

  • The new View is framed on the object (Normal lens, Standing eye, on the side facing you), and it appears selected, with its camera and frustum. The Card opens on the new Presentation.
  • Your Stage standpoint does not move. To see what the View sees, Look through, as for any View.
  • In Plan nothing changes: Present this arms Place a View aimed at the object.

28Scrub on the cards, with waits and keys

Kept, and delivered by §33 A: PR #271 shipped a second, separate scrub bar. §33 retires it for this one segmented track.

  • The rail under the cards is one track. Each card's progress rail and each Travel gutter's line join into a single scrubber. Press on it and drag: the playhead runs across cards and moves continuously. A tooltip gives the time and where it is (0:42 · Stop 3 · Doorway). Pressing a card's picture still starts from that Stop.
  • Time isn't proportional on the cards, and they say so. Every Stop is one card wide, so a card's rail is that Stop's own stay, and a Travel gutter is its own short track. Proportional time moves to the playhead's tooltip and the time readout.
  • A wait shows where it happens. In a Manual Guide (§31) every card ends its rail with a ⏸ cap. Playing stops there and says Waits for Next — ▶ continues, as the visitor would press Next.
  • Keys: the strip is a slider. ←/→ move 1 s, Shift 5 s, PgUp/PgDn go to the previous or next Stop, and Home/End go to the ends. Space plays.

31Follow the Guide, or step aside and come back

Amended by §33: pacing is now edited on the Guide strip (§33 A), the visitor bar gains segmented progress (§33 F), and the welcome moves to the Experience Card (§33 B). The model and journeys A–H are unchanged.

Think of a museum guide. Visitors can follow along, or step aside to look closer and rejoin the group where it is. Pacing is one choice for the whole Guide. Exploring is the visitor's time, never the Guide's. An invitation is a hint; it never stops the tour.

Guide · Automatic or ManualOrdered Stops and the transitions between them, and the one clock. Automatic moves on when each stay ends; Manual waits for Next at every Stop.
StopOne visit: a Presentation, its entry View, a stay (Automatic only), how it arrives, and an optional invitation to explore.
PresentationOrdered optional Views joined by Cut or Travel. The order is a suggestion. Listed in Places opens it without a Guide.
Explore (visitor only)Freezes the Guide at its playhead. Visitors step through Views or look around. Back restores the playhead and whether the tour was playing. Never saved.
Camera (unchanged)Views, paths, path time and evaluation. Every use of a path shares it. A Cut never creates one.
