Camera paths show where they go

Example
3D

  • Travel is a 3px ribbon with a white casing and white chevrons every ~35px pointing toward the arrival View; a hollow ring marks where it leaves. Leaf when on the tour, gold when selected.
  • Cut is a quiet dotted ink link; its ✂ chip appears only on hover or selection.

Turn a View by its frustum

  • Press anywhere in the selected View's frustum and drag: the View turns about its eye. Height, distance and lens stay. A dashed arc shows the turn, with the angle.
  • The dot moves the View. Shift snaps the turn to 15°. Release is one change and one Undo, and nothing is said.
  • In 3D, the selected camera's frustum lines turn it the same way, on the floor.

Present this places a camera you can see

  • The new View is framed on the object (Normal lens, Standing eye, on the side facing you), and it appears selected, with its camera and frustum. The Card opens on the new Presentation.
  • Your Stage standpoint does not move. To see what the View sees, Look through, as for any View.
  • In Plan nothing changes: Present this arms Place a View aimed at the object.

What should a visitor see first?

  • A ready draft. The Experience lens is ready the moment you enter it: an unnamed draft and the start card. Nothing is saved yet, so looking around never dirties the project or adds an Undo step. The first real step (Capture, Present an object, Place a View) creates the Experience and its first Presentation as one change, and one Undo takes both back.
  • An Experiences group at the top of the Index, the way a design file lists its pages. It uses the Index's own language: the small uppercase group title with a count, 28 px sans rows, gold tint on the current one, ••• on hover for Rename, Duplicate and Delete. The + is a quiet ink icon at the end of the group title, no ring or fill, the same treatment every Index group can use later. One click switches, and there is no menu to open. Many Experiences: the group shows at most 3 rows by default: the current one first, then the two most recently opened. Below them, "Show all 14" opens the whole list in its authored order, capped at 8 rows (about 224 px) with its own scroll, and a filter field appears once there are more than 8. "Show less" folds it again, and the choice is remembered in this browser. The Presentations group below never moves more than 3 rows down.
  • Creating happens in place. + adds a row at the end of the list with its name selected and a gold underline, the same as renaming a Presentation. Enter keeps it, Esc removes it. The new Experience opens empty with the start card. No dialog, no popup Card.

Environment: a sky, a horizon and light, Paper by default

Preset
Seen in
EnvironmentWorld · saved

Sky, horizon and light. Rooms, floors and your own lights stay yours.

While editing, the Stage shows

Picking a preset is one change with one Undo. Preview, snapshots and the published visit always use it.
Scope: a decent, bounded round (R5), about the size of one Card redesign. The look is a fixed table of presets. The work is one shared environment component, one saved Scene field and one picker. Nothing in Layout changes, so rooms and floors stay editable on their own.

In R5

  • Four presets: Paper (default), Daylight, Golden hour, Dusk. Each fixes a sky gradient, ground colour and horizon haze, hemisphere fill, one sun (direction, colour, soft shadow) and exposure.
  • One runtime-safe preset table and one environment component, shared by the Stage, snapshots, Preview and the published visit. No editor module reaches the visitor chunk.
  • Scene saves environment: { preset } as World presentation. The Scene format number goes up, older files are refused, and fixtures are regenerated.
  • The picker sits in the World Inspector when nothing is selected. "Plain Paper while editing" is a Stage preference and isn't saved.

Later, not now

  • Custom colours, sun position and time-of-day sliders
  • Image-based lighting (HDRI), clouds, weather, an animated sky
  • Changing the environment per Stop or during a tour (Experience)
  • Fog and render quality as their own controls

Cost and risk

  • The sky is one gradient dome and the ground one disc that fades into the haze: two draw calls.
  • The sun is the only new shadow caster. Today only authored lights cast shadows on the Stage. The sun joins the existing on-demand shadow invalidation, so it re-renders on change, not every frame. Its shadow camera fits the Layout bounds.
  • Authored lights add on top. Dusk is dim on purpose, so a room's own lamps carry it.
  • Paper stays the default. A new World looks exactly as it does now, and Preview shows the same Paper without the grid (never black).
  • On the Stage an environment keeps Paper's bounded sheet and its grid; the sky, the ground around the sheet and the light change, and the sun's shadows land on the sheet. Grid on or off applies as on Paper. Visitors and snapshots never see the grid.
  • Presets are stylized, not photographic: flat gradients, soft shadows, warm paper-like tones, the same art direction as the shell.

A Biskiq cursor set

One family: a forest-ink shape with a white rim, readable on Paper, on the dark mat and on every environment. A gold badge means "this adds something". Hover each tile to try its cursor.

Hover the Stage: empty Paper, the object, the selected View's dot and frustum, the path.

  • Selection is the arrow: on empty Stage, on objects, on Views, on buttons and in the Index. Things you can click to select never turn the cursor into a hand.
  • The hand means grabbing: the open hand only while Space is held, or over something you drag as a whole, and the closed hand only while a drag is under way (pan, orbit, a dragged point).
  • Each gesture says what it will do: turn over a selected frustum, move over a selected View's dot, add point over a selected path, place View while Place a View is armed, look while looking through, and not allowed where a press would be refused.
  • A gesture hint is a small hint tag beside the cursor, never a toast. It hides after the first use in a session.
  • Build: one cursors.css with --cursor-* properties, each url(data:image/svg+xml,…) x y, <system fallback>, at 32 px with a 2× image-set. The Stage's data-cursor uses these names.

Card
Camera paths (Camera owns them; Travel uses them)
  • Compact Stop cards use forest material, a numbered picture and a single line title. Repeated visits include their entry View. Time appears on the rail, the clock and the connection. The first Stop starts the Guide and the last ends it.
  • The Guide's own settings live on the strip. Press its name to rename it in place (Enter keeps, Esc cancels). ⋯ offers Remove Guide. Pacing is the pill, order is the cards, playback is ▶ and the rail. With nothing selected, the Card is the Experience Card.
  • Quiet selection: a raised forest fill and a thin light ring, never gold. ✦ (invites) and +N (Views to explore) stay, at reduced contrast.
  • Between Stops: ✂ for a Cut and ↗ 5 s for a Travel, with the path's real time. Pressing a chip opens its Transition Card. There is no manual + Add path: choosing Travel makes the path.
  • One timeline. Each card's bottom rail is that Stop's stay, and each Travel gutter's rail is that Travel. A Cut takes no time, so its gutter shows a dotted hairline and the playhead jumps over it. One playhead, one clock (0:21 / 0:53 in the header) and one scrub interaction. Cards keep fixed widths, so the scrub maps each segment on its own: dragging across a 4 s card and a 12 s card moves at different rates, and the tooltip names the time and the Stop.
  • Manual: every card rail is a wait that ends in ⏸. Play stops there, and ▶ continues, as a visitor presses Next. A wait has no length on the clock, so the header reads 0:09 + 5 waits.
  • Pacing lives on the strip. The pill opens a small popover with the two choices. Arrow keys move, Enter chooses, Esc closes, and focus returns to the pill. There is no other pacing control.
  • Reorder: drag a card (on touch, hold it first), or use Alt+←/→. A thin light insertion line, with no gold border, shows where it lands. A card's outgoing Cut or Travel moves with it, because the choice belongs to the Stop it leaves. Two rules keep that predictable, with nothing dormant. The last Stop has no outgoing step at all, so a Stop moved to the end loses its Travel; moved back into the middle, it leaves by a fresh Cut, and no hidden Travel settings come back. And a Travel whose two Stops now show the same View becomes a Cut. Both are written in the same change, said once in the feedback line, and leave the Camera path in place for the next Travel. Overflow scrolls sideways with no visible scrollbar and pages with the arrows, and the selected or playing Stop scrolls into view.

Where the name shows

  • One source of truth. label: string | null. null means the name is the linked object's live name, read through the stable subject reference, never looked up by name. A string is the creator's own name. Nothing stores a copy of the object's name and calls it live.
  • The name shows everywhere it's used: the Index, the Presentation Card, the Guide's Stop cards, the Stop and Transition Cards, the visitor's caption and Places. Typing a name makes it the creator's own, and Use the object's name goes back to following.
  • Identity stays separate from the name. The kind line reads about Pleyel piano · Form 2, with Show in World. Link and Unlink are explicit actions, one Undo each. Capture suggests the object under the centre of the View and waits for Link. Pick on the Stage arms a one-shot picker, and Esc cancels it.
  • Unlinking keeps the current name as the Presentation's own. Environment and region Presentations stay unlinked; nothing forces a subject on them.
  • World shows nothing about the link. No badge, outline, warning or special state marks an object because a Presentation is about it, in the plan, the Stage or the object's Card. The relationship is managed from the Presentation (About, Show in World).
  • Removing the object just works. The World removal is one change that also unlinks the Presentation and keeps its last name as its own. The Presentation, its Views and its Stops stay. There's no World warning, no removed state and no repair; the World's feedback is the ordinary Pleyel piano removed., and one Undo brings the object and the link back. Nothing ever retargets.

Travel and Camera paths

  • Choosing Travel adds a straight Camera path if the View pair has none, in the same change and Undo.
  • Reordering Stops, changing an entry View or reordering Presentation Views reconciles every affected Travel automatically.
  • Travel becomes Cut when both Stops show the same View or when the Stop becomes last. Its Camera path stays available for reuse.
  • Imported duplicate paths require a deliberate repair. Problems names the pair and opens its Transition Card.

Try it in the strip above: drag North window (Stop 3) to the end. Stop 2's Travel now leads to the piano's second visit, so a straight Front ↔ From the window path is added in the same Undo. The custom Front ↔ North window path stays, unused, for when you move the Stop back. Nothing is stretched or retargeted.

Visitor Preview

Run

  • One thin segmented bar in the HUD: one segment per Stop occurrence, so a second visit to the Pleyel piano is its own segment. Done segments are filled, upcoming ones are faint, and the current one fills with its stay.
  • Travel fills the short connector between two segments. A Cut has a hairline gap and nothing to fill.
  • Manual shows the current segment outlined, with Next when you're ready, and no fill, because there's no time to show.
  • Pause and Explore freeze it. Explore dims the bar while the explore strip is up. Back to the tour restores the same fill and the same play, pause or wait. Back's 2 s lead-in shows as the fill stepping back a little.
  • Segments are equal widths with a minimum, not proportional to time, so a 2 s stay is still visible. The final segment fills, and the end card follows.
  • Progress reflects the running tour. Travel fills the connector first; the destination stay remains empty until arrival, then fills from zero. Pause and Explore freeze the stay. A recorded visit does not complete a stay.

Feedback and Problems

Feedback line · typed, not guessed from the text
Problems · live from the state above
Imported file
  • Feedback has three treatments. A successful change shows a brief forest-green line. Information is neutral. A refusal stays visible long enough to read.
  • Problems are written for the creator: Stop numbers, the Guide's name, Presentation names and View labels, in a sentence that names the real fault (a missing or doubled path, the same View on both sides, a removed object), never points at things that are gone. Each has a Show that opens the control that fixes it. Ids, codes and source paths sit behind Copy details.
  • Ordinary edits reconcile Camera paths automatically. Problems provides a repair route for imported or hand-edited invalid data.
