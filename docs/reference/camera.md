# Camera — views, connectivity and evaluation

**Owns:** Views, spatial connectivity, routes, framing envelopes, projection policy, intrinsic
movement profiles, evaluation and reusable Camera resources. Experience owns editorial occurrences,
order, holds and pacing, and maps invocation time through Camera.

## One authority

`@portfolio/camera-core` is the single Camera authority for connectivity, routes, framing,
projection and evaluation. `camera-route.ts` and `camera-motion.ts` are its current mechanism;
every profile controller — guided travel, free look, reduced motion, film, future XR — realizes
viewing intent through it. No second navigation graph, evaluator or independent pose/FOV
interpolation exists.

- Durable scene/runtime types and camera-graph construction live in `@portfolio/project-model`;
  `camera-core` consumes only structural camera-graph inputs.
- The visitor cold runtime evaluates released documents through the same authority.
- The public `/p/:publicationId` route receives an honest 404 under strict source admission until T4.
- The mechanism is replaceable implementation, not the motion ceiling. Travel, orbit, track,
  lens/focus and retimed invocation can extend the vocabulary. A controller never supplies a second
  pose/FOV interpolation authority.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Camera node / View | Eye / target / FOV, world-local. `roomId` is absent; the legacy room-local form is not admitted. |
| Connection | Edge between Views. JSON stores **interior** anchors only. |
| Path kinds | `rounded-polyline` · `auto-bezier` (no tangent handles) |
| Order | Experience Guide order. Camera connectivity never determines editorial order. |
| View | Camera-owned spatial attention: pose/framing (or explicitly adaptive subject framing), projection and evaluation |

Defaults: eye **1.65 m**, target **1.25 m**, distance **3 m**, clearance **0.35 m**.

## Authored versus generated anchors

Only **interior** anchors are authored and persisted on a Connection. The endpoint specifier
`node:<id>:position` is generated by the resolver at runtime, never authored and never persisted as
an interior. Node-view endpoints are likewise generated at runtime for the visitor and release
path; a compiled delivery descriptor may carry generated endpoints, but there is never a second
authored set of connection anchors. Where authored anchor data is read, its Y is preserved
byte-for-byte; nothing in the product rewrites it.

## Connectivity policy

The Camera unit uses the `independent-components` policy through the one named
`CAMERA_UNIT_SCENE_VALIDATION` constant, which Camera-unit admission and its runtime Scene
projection force after merging host catalogue options. One Camera unit may carry independent Travel
pairs (A–B and C–D); they need no bridge edge, and a Cut authorizes no Camera edge. A direct Scene
document keeps its single-component default. A Travel must resolve to exactly one existing
connection joining the two Views, in either direction. A missing connection is a typed gap, never a
fabricated path. Experience lowering reports gaps and ambiguous connections, and binds only Camera
identities. No Experience edit deletes, retargets or reshapes a Camera connection.

## Framing envelopes and motion guards

A directional view track may carry one optional `framingEnvelope` per travel direction:
`0 ≤ enterStart ≤ enterEnd ≤ exitStart ≤ exitEnd ≤ 1`. Route construction selects and deep-copies
only the oriented direction's envelope; motion samples its enter/exit smootherstep ramps against
edge-local distance progress and applies the resulting weight to both Cartesian target and FOV.
A missing envelope preserves full authored framing; envelopes on tracks without authored keys
remain automatic.

Motion creation compiles stateless minimum-standoff, POI angular-rate and hazardous late-exit bypass
guards; runtime seeks apply those target-only corrections deterministically. Larger FOV is wider /
zoomed out; smaller FOV is tighter / zoomed in.

Verified dense invariants, over whole transitions and at arbitrary seeks:

- motion stays finite with `distance(target, eye) ≥ VISITOR_CAMERA_PROJECTION.near`;
- canonical node poses at `p = 0/1` in every branch (forward keys, reversed keys,
  reversed-no-key `travelFacingEnds`, no-envelope, automatic no-key);
- deterministic great-circle fallback on over-capacity and near-antipodal remaps;
- no adjacent 180° pops;
- bounded angular rate wherever compiled rate limiting applies, with peak measured from the
  compiled segment pairs — never the nominal policy constant;
- bypass/standoff guards change only the target; the double-whip bypass turns on exactly at the
  policy off-axis, path-excess and angular-rate thresholds;
- FOV rides the same envelope weight as the target and is never changed by target-only guards.

One exception is deliberate, not a defect: **zero-width envelope ramps**
(`enterStart = enterEnd` or `exitStart = exitEnd`) are legal intentional steps. They are exempt
from the smooth-ramp rate assertion and must still be deterministic, finite, non-degenerate and
exact on both sides of the bound.

Dense whole-transition sweeps in the heavy lane hold these invariants. Timing is asserted relatively
on identical input, never as a wall-clock floor.

## Guide time map

The Guide time map is one pure derived read in `packages/camera-core/src/guide-time-map.ts`: a
Stop's stay is authored seconds or a Manual wait, Cut occupies zero elapsed seconds, and Travel
carries Camera's exact directed motion. It is the only source for the Guide strip, tour time, each
Stop time, scrub and playback; it owns no frame loop, state or serialized field. Reduced motion
settles to the destination without changing authored seconds.

The editor's ephemeral playback session is host view state: an accepted edit invalidates it, a
refused edit does not, and nothing it does enters F4, history or Save. Preview opens the welcome
chooser for a guided Experience, starts at the Guide's first Stop (or the host-chosen Preview
start), and realizes guided/free/Places/return/end through the same interpreter and Camera sampling
the visitor uses. Visitor chunks import no editor module.

## Preview ticket and visitor parity

In the editor, Preview entry and return run through the F3 Camera-output ticket:
`enterPreview`, `returnPreview`, `cancelPreview`. The visitor surface is built when the Experience
lens opens and stays hidden, inert and on-demand between sessions, so an entry starts a run over
the already-built scene and issues no draw while hidden. Editor Preview starts paused; public entry
retains autoplay. A return validates the current source/output context and refuses with a reason
otherwise. Exactly one Camera output owner exists per viewport/session.

Preview and prepared publication share semantic lowering and evaluation with isolated sessions; the
visitor Preview is the same runtime under the same evaluation. Runtime-safe domain evaluation is
shared; editor selection, history, gizmos and authoring machinery are not.

## Views and Experience references

A View is Camera-owned spatial attention: pose/framing or explicitly adaptive subject framing,
projection and evaluation. A Presentation or Stop may reference a View, supply supported entry
context and invocation time mapping, or deliberately keep the current viewpoint; it never copies
Camera truth. A missing referenced View is a repairable unresolved binding, distinct from
intentional keep-viewpoint. Transient World inspection becomes an authored View only through
explicit capture.

**Cues follow their source.** Camera emits its own progress markers; performances own their cues;
Experience owns the visitor-facing interaction bindings that listen to them. Spatial Camera does not
become the owner of every performance event, and Experience bindings evaluate against canonical
evaluation, never a copied timeline.
