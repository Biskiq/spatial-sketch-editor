The final T2 design for light on the Stage, the Light Cards, Time, Sky and the Celestial Dial, Artwork framing and placement, and how they sit in the real editor shell. Revision 4, 13 November 2026. Design only. PR #314 implements the earlier fix.md design on this branch; §7.4 lists what it ships and what this sheet changes. 0 · Status and authority

normative design

This sheet is the design authority for T2 lighting presentation, the Light Cards, the Environment control (Time and Sky), and the shell placement of these controls. Where it disagrees with the documents listed below, this sheet wins; everywhere else fix.md stays the contract, including its diagnosis, its execution order and its §12 record of what PR #314 implemented. fix.md carries the same rule in its status note. The other contracts are not edited by this sheet. Each change they need is listed in §7 and lands with the slice that implements it.

Decided in this revision

  1. Light guides are ray silhouettes. The selected Spot shows its source, aim axis, two to four silhouette rays to the target and a planar cap. No gold footprints are wrapped onto surfaces. The dense variant appears only while a shape is being edited, or when Show influence is on.
  2. Brightness is one logarithmic slider from ⅛× to 8×. Zero and out-of-range stored values are preserved until the user actually writes.
  3. Beam is one dual-scrub row with a live diagram. It shows full cone width, 2·angle; Advanced shows the stored half-angle. The outer thumbs set the width and the notches under the track set Soft edge, so the two pairs never overlap. Every light Card keeps four everyday rows (§3).
  4. Colour is edited on the Card. A White | Colour view switch swaps the warmth ramp for Hue and Colour amount tracks; the wheel opens the restored radial picker. Switching writes nothing (§3).
  5. Emitters are dimensional abstract glyphs (sphere with arcs for Point, barrel for Spot), shown only when Lights is armed, hovered or selected. Idle lights draw nothing, but their invisible pick target stays live (§2).
  6. The Environment control lives at the Stage's bottom-right, apart from the dock: a World-status instrument that shows the Sun or Moon, the preset and the time, and expands upward into the Sky panel (candidate C, chosen over A and B by the shell comparison in §4.1).
  7. The Celestial Dial is the one Stage instrument for Sky. A floor sundial inside a quiet elliptical bearing ring, with one Sun or Moon glyph on today's active arc. Glyph, beam and shadow show the same Time and heading. No rim mark and no second marker (§4.2).
  8. The Sun's position is a pure function of Time, heading and arc height, so every Time maps to exactly one position. Stage gestures stay on the current arc; only the Time dial wraps (§4.3).
  9. Directional light is retired. The Sky owns the only parallel light. Refusing legacy projects and the format bump are a separate future slice (§7.3).

How to read

  • Each section starts with live prototypes and ends with spec tables: dimensions, states, gestures and keys, fields, boundaries and contract changes.
  • Tags: now can ship with fields that already exist · later T2 needs a T2 slice · WebGPU waits for the renderer refactor · record history, kept only to explain a decision.
  • Ink on the Stage: solid = shows an authored quantity · dashed = derived or reference · faded = hidden behind geometry · gold = the held grip or the selected subject · cobalt = proposal or ghost · red = refusal.
  • Selected UI controls are deep forest and hover is moss, as shipped. Cards stay white.

1 · Identity and marks

kept, refined

This keeps the shipped character: a forest shell, white Cards, parchment and moss accents, and gold used sparingly. Nothing is remapped. The refinement is in restraint: there are fewer marks on the Stage, every mark has one meaning, and no surface-wrapped gold appears.

The emitter at actual Stage size

Drawn 1:1 (zoom the page to 100% to judge). The emitter is an abstract light source, not a lamp: a white instrument body with one ink outline and a soft contact shadow, and only the lens or core in the light's own colour, as a flat swatch with a glint. Point is a sphere with four short radiating arcs; Spot is a short barrel turned along its aim, its lens face toward the target. They stay distinct at 18 px because their silhouettes differ (round and rayed against long and directional), not only their detail. Restored from the 68b1b3f Aperture study; the flat "sticker" disc of revision 2 is withdrawn.

Default guide vs dense variant

The dense variant adds rays, the soft-edge ring at angle × (1 − penumbra) and the cap's far arc; a Point adds the floor reach ring. It explains influence, but it is never the default: at rest it would compete with the room and the art.

Stress cases

Selected guide over a future volumetric shaft

Room
Shaft

Result

  • Without the casing, the guide disappears twice. In a dusk or night room, dark ink falls below 2:1 contrast against the walls. Inside a strong shaft, the rays sit on a moving bright texture. Turn the casing off to see both.
  • With the casing (a light stroke 2.4 px wider than the ink, at 70%, under every guide line), the rays and axis read in all three rooms and over both shaft strengths. It adds no colour and no glow.
  • The shaft and the guide disagree, and that is correct. The shaft fades with distance and dust density, so its visible length is shorter than the target distance. The guide always ends at the target distance. Neither is derived from the other.
  • The shaft field (density and dust) lives on the light or the World, not on the guide. It is listed as unresolved in §8, because it waits for WebGPU behind S0.

3 · Light Cards

fields exist

These are the same white Cards, drawn at their true 288 px width (252 px at ≤860 px). Continuous controls use a stacked row: the label and value on one line, then a full-width control below. At 288 px, an inline 74px | slider | value row leaves about 134 px for the slider, and labelled stops collide. A stacked row gives 264 px. Both Cards keep four everyday rows: Brightness, Colour, Beam or Reach, Shadow. Colour editing and the Beam diagram replace content inside their row; they never add rows.

True-size QA (measured live in this page)

Beam: one dual-scrub row with its diagram chosen. The track is the beam in section, centred on the aim. The outer pair of thumbs sits on the track and sets the full width. The inner pair are notches under the track and set where full brightness ends. The two pairs live on separate rows, so they can never overlap, even at 0% soft edge. Above, a true-angle diagram redraws live: dotted outer edges, the solid moss core, and the stepped falloff between them. The track carries the same profile, so the two read as one thing. Pointer: a thumb or notch hit picks it; otherwise the upper half drives Beam and the lower half Soft edge. Each pair is mirrored, so either side drags it.
Colour: White and Colour are views of one field chosen. The switch in the row's header swaps the content: White shows the warmth ramp; Colour shows a wheel button and two thin tracks, Hue and Colour amount. Switching writes nothing. Each view remembers its own values while the Card is open. When the view does not produce the stored colour, its thumbs are hollow and the readout says "Colour kept" or "White kept"; the first drag or key writes. The wheel button, or the swatch in the Card's header, opens the radial picker.

Retired: the (a)/(b) comparison of revision 3. The overlaid native range inputs of (a) collided below about 40% soft edge; (b) cost a fifth row. Splitting the two pairs vertically keeps (a)'s single row and its diagram, and loses the collision.

Advanced (Spot)

  • Only one group is open at a time. Each closed group shows its summary on the right.
  • Typed fields commit on Enter and restore on Esc. Blur writes nothing. A value outside the range is refused at the field with the reason.
  • Shape shows the stored half-angle with its full-width echo, so the Card's Beam and the stored field can never be confused.
  • Brightness holds the exact intensity in candela, including 0 and values outside the slider.
  • Colour holds the exact stored hex and shows it both ways: as white (on the ramp or not) and as hue and amount. There is no mode field to write.
  • Position › Aim toward takes a point and writes rotation only. The point shown is derived, 3 m along the current aim; no target is stored.
  • Shadow › Softness is disabled: no per-light field exists. It is listed as a later contract change.
  • There is no Direction group: Directional is retired.

4 · Environment: Time, Sky and the Celestial Dial

S1 · S2

Sky is global World state, not a tool and not an object. It gets its own instrument at the Stage's bottom-right corner: at rest a small window onto the World's sky (the Sun or Moon at its height, the preset and the time), and on press a compact Sky panel that grows upward and inward from that corner. It never arms, never selects and never becomes a Card.

Build stage
Before S1 the control and panel keep their full composition. Only environment.preset is live; Time, Sun direction and Haze are drawn as placeholders, never as working-looking controls.

4.2 · The Celestial Dial: one Stage instrument for Sun, Moon, bearing and shadow

View
Furniture

One instrument at the World mark. A floor plate with a gnomon and its shadow sits inside a quiet elliptical bearing ring: the ring is 360° of direction, not an orbit. The one Sun or Moon glyph sits on the ring at its bearing; only today's active arc is drawn, bounded by sunrise and sunset (or moonrise and moonset). The glyph, the dashed beam to the gnomon top and the shadow lie on one line, so position, bearing and shadow all show the same authored Time and heading. Drag the glyph or the shadow tip along the arc for Time; drag the noon mark, where the noon shadow falls, to turn the arc (heading). Twilight is reached only through the Time dial in Sky.

The function is pure: no date, latitude or clock is involved, and the same project always renders the same Sun.

kept from #314
Width
  • The selection is a 1 px gold outline 7 px outside the frame, so thin frames stay visible.
  • Size tags stay on while the Artwork is selected; a corner drag adds the framed size.
  • Card order is Size, Hang, Frame, then actions. Position and rotation live under Advanced.

Moving along walls: ends, openings and corners

Pointer
  • The Artwork follows the pointer while its whole frame backs onto one wall.
  • Where it would overhang a wall end, an opening or a corner, it stops at the last place it fitted: Stops at the wall edge.
  • Over a gap, a doorway or the floor, it waits where it was. Once it fits on the next wall, it jumps there and takes that wall's direction.
  • Shift at press moves it freely in its own plane. Flat Artwork tangent to a curved Wall stays valid.
  • Release commits the final candidate from the pointer-up event, even when a preview frame was still queued.

8 · Recommendations, open decisions, handoff

Recommendations

  1. Ship the ray guide, the emitter pick target and the stacked Card rows together. They use only existing fields, and they remove today's two worst readings: the invisible light and the clipped Card.
  2. Put the Environment control at the Stage's bottom-right now, with the placeholder panel and live preset tiles, so S2 adds dials without moving anything.
  3. Run S0's tone-mapping decision before tuning Brightness stops. The stops are a UI mapping, so stored values survive.
  4. Build the Celestial Dial in S2 as one localized instrument with constant screen size; keep it out of selection, the Card and the visitor runtime.
  5. Pick up the Directional retirement as its own small Tier 2 PR.

Unresolved owner decisions

  1. The final preset set, and how it maps to today's daylight, golden-hour, dusk and the neutral preset.
  2. Whether the Sun always casts shadows (PLATE L980).
  3. Moon strength and colour under More.
  4. Default Shadow state for a new Spot.
  5. The volumetric field: on the light, on the World, or both (S0-gated).
  6. Group Card scope for light rigs (G1).
  7. Where Show influence lives: ⚙ viewing options or a Card foot action.
