Lighting visual language · exploration

design only · not implementation authority

An open look at how Biskiq draws light: icons, the emitter in the Stage, beam and reach guides, future source shapes and the Card. It questions the shipped circle, cone and Instrument glyphs instead of assuming them. Nothing here is in production, and nothing here adds a light kind, a field or a control. Section 7 separates what could improve #314 now from future T2 work and research.

The rule that holds everything together: a guide shows where light can reach; only the rendered picture shows what it does. Every mark below is line-only mark ink. Nothing glows, nothing is tinted with the light's colour, and nothing gets brighter where the light is stronger (T2 §5, fix.md §4.1).

1 · FixtureThe visible object: a can, a tube, a shade. Part of the Scene, chosen from the Library, grouped with its light. S8 · G1
2 · EmitterThe logical light: the Scene light entity with position, direction and shape. Visitors never see it. Point · Spot · Directional today
3 · Stage markWhat the editor draws so the emitter can be found, read and handled: glyph, guides, grips. Derived and never serialized. this section
4 · LightThe rendered result: illumination, falloff, shadows. This is the only layer that ever looks like light. renderer

In context ·

A · Instrument shipped base

The shell's open 1.5 px forms. Today's Lights tool is a bulb; Brightness, Colour, Shape and Shadows are the Advanced group heads shipped in 0923555e.

Keep: grid, stroke and rhythm. Problems: the bulb is a fixture; Brightness and Sun are the same glyph (shell-icons.ts lightBrightness = sun); Spot looks like Shape; nothing in the set grows into line or panel sources.

B · Aperture recommended

Every light kind is drawn as its emitter shape plus how light leaves it: rays all round for a point, an aperture for a spot, parallel arrows for distant light, a bar or panel for extended sources.

Why: the toolbar, Library, Index, Card and Stage glyph all use the same forms; the Sun and Brightness collision goes away; it grows naturally into line and panel sources; and it keeps Instrument's 24 grid and 1.5 round stroke. Cost: the Point rays still look like a generic "brightness" glyph outside Biskiq.

C · Fixture rejected

Pictures of lamps: bare bulb, track can, batten, troffer, pendant; dimmer, gel, barn doors.

Instantly familiar to lighting people, but it makes the emitter look like a fixture, which is the confusion this brief is meant to prevent. Once S8 fixture models exist, the Library would show a "Pendant" light icon next to a Pendant object. The heavier strokes also clash with the 1.5 px shell.

D · Photometric later, Advanced only

Polar intensity curves, like an IES data sheet: a circle for a point, a lobe for a spot, a batwing for a pendant.

Accurate and distinctive, and it would carry IES data later. At 16 px the Spot lobe reads as a pin or a drop and the Point is just a circle. Worth keeping for one future Distribution group head, not as the main family.

Should it always be a point?

  • Point and Spot stay screen-space glyphs of constant size (20 px, fix.md §4.2). The emitter really is a point, and its glyph has to stay readable at any zoom.
  • The glyph's outline shows the kind of emission. A Point has rays all round. A Spot has an aperture lip: a short open arc facing the aim, in place of today's filled wedge, which reads as a cursor or a play button.

What each guide is for

  • Instrument: the #314 grammar. A ring at the target perpendicular to the axis, two silhouette edges, the inner soft-edge ring, and grips. Its maths is stable, so it remains the editing tool.
  • Landing trace: where the beam edge meets real surfaces, broken across the corner between wall and floor. It answers "what does this reach?" better than a ring floating in space, but it has no grips: dragging a line that bends across creases jumps.
  • Protractor: a fixed-size fan at the glyph showing beam width and soft edge as angles. It works at any scale, but its screen angle is only approximate, so the tag carries the exact number.
  • Adaptive: when selected, the Instrument plus a faint landing trace. When the footprint is under 14 px or off-screen, the Protractor takes the Beam and Soft-edge grips, so a spot can still be edited in a diorama or a hall.

Switch With rendered pool: the pool is the light; the guides stay ink. Under Adaptive, Model and Hall both move the grips to the Protractor.

Six scenarios, each split into the four layers in section view. Each one is marked now, approximate (existing emitters plus objects), future T2 (a WebGL feature Biskiq does not author yet), or research (needs renderer capability Biskiq does not have). RectAreaLightWebGLEmits evenly from a rectangle: good for tubes, strips and panels. 6 · Recommended direction

Aperture: one vocabulary from the toolbar to the Stage

  1. Icons: family B on Instrument's grid (24, 1.5 round). Light kinds are emitter shape plus emission. Property glyphs reuse the Stage's own marks: Beam is the protractor, Reach is the reach bar, Softness is the falloff profile, and Aim is the target grip.
  2. Emitter glyph: Point keeps its rays; Spot swaps the filled wedge for an aperture lip; future Line and Panel are world-size outlines with a facing tick. The Sun keeps the dial.
  3. Guides: the #314 Instrument stays the editing tool. A faint landing trace shows where the beam meets surfaces. A protractor at the source takes over when the footprint is too small or off-screen.
  4. Groups and fixtures: one aggregate glyph per light group with a count badge, and member glyphs on the second click (G1). A fixture never changes the emitter glyph.
  5. Card: a kind glyph in the head; no new everyday rows. Shape information that only exists for future kinds goes in Advanced › Shape.

Trade-offs accepted: the Point rays look like the generic brightness glyph outside Biskiq (inside Biskiq, Brightness gets its own arcs); a two-state Spot guide (instrument plus trace) is more ink than #314 alone, so the trace sits at 35 % and only on selection.

Rejected: glow orbs and coloured glyphs (they imitate light); fixture pictograms as light icons (they confuse emitter and fixture); isophote or brightness-contour rings (they show intensity, which only the render may do); a filled pattern image inside the footprint (it looks lit); three great circles for Point reach (clutter); separate cones for each strip segment (they suggest several emitters); dragging the Beam on the landing trace (it jumps at creases).
