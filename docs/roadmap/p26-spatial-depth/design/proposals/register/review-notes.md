**Design artifact only · unratified · 2026-09-22.** The product specification is [direction.md](./direction.md); [evidence.md](./evidence.md) records the inspected baseline. No P26 product acceptance is claimed. Completion here means *review-ready*, not ratified or implementation-ready.

It re-derived the earlier review's leads against the current branch instead of applying them as a fix list, rejected the ones the code disproved, found defects the earlier pass did not report, and changed the atlas, its specification and its verification.

## 1. What is verified, and how

Five independent layers, all of which can be re-run by a reviewer:

1. **Executable consistency matrix.** `node docs/roadmap/p26-spatial-depth/design/proposals/register/check-atlas.mjs` renders every board, then asserts the scenario matrix in §2 below: the durable view control, the Navigator row, the status reference and the Inspector identity must all describe the same canonical source. It also probes every displayed control for a real handler and fails on any control that changes no state, selection or message.
2. **Disclosed geometry.** The same checker asserts that each board draws the geometry its own narration claims — that the two S1/S2 ceiling gaps are dimensioned, that the C-SPAN underside is drawn at 3.55/4.55 with a 0.745 gap, that a 3D board draws exactly one floor datum and the canonical wall and ceiling planes, that C-SPAN's board does *not* draw the generated closure it replaces, that the footprint board draws a 7 × 3.22 m region overlapping a 7 × 3.28 m region, that the curved elevation is foreshortened *and* changes with the canonical curve, that the excluded-selection board keeps the source its narration says stays, and that an edge-on source exposes no fields of the plane it is perpendicular to.
3. **Live-renderer layout audit.** Measurements below are taken from the real renderer, not estimated, because a zero-clipping claim measured from estimates is not evidence. Run in the app's browser with every board loaded:

   ```js
   const boxes=[...document.querySelector('#frame .canvas svg').querySelectorAll('text')]
     .map(t=>({b:t.getBBox(),txt:t.textContent}));
   // flag b.x < -1.5, b.x+b.width > 772+1.5, b.y outside 0..738, bbox pairs overlapping >45% of the smaller box,
   // and #frame .navrow / .inspector .property / .status span / .context / .ranges with scrollWidth > clientWidth.
   ```
   **Result after the corrections: clean on all 29 boards** — no clipped text, no overlapping labels, no shell overflow. The same run before the corrections found 12 defects (§4). The page also has no horizontal scroll at 1280 × 800, the frame scales to 1240 px, and the 1:1 toggle restores a 1440 px stage slot.

   A second live measurement covers the case bounding boxes cannot see: **a line drawn through a label**. Every stroked element (path, line, rect outline) is sampled with `getPointAtLength` and tested against every text box. It found the prop's leader label running 98 px across the curved partition and the east exterior wall on all seven non-mirrored Plan boards, the cut line through `North · R-NORTH` / `South · R-SOUTH` on the three cut-range boards, the east-gallery outline through `O-WINDOW`, the draft guide through the C-SPAN label, and the demo outline through the `W-CURVE` label. 4. **Scripted journeys in the live page.** Eight end-to-end journeys, driven through the real controls (see §5).

## 4. Found independently in this pass

- **Cross-fixture identity leak.** On the reveal board, the Inspector rendered the S4 window `O-N4` as "Gallery door / Opening · W-SHARED" with the S1 door's dimensions and host. It now has its own state: Gallery window, room N4, membership, range depth and a note that the wing is a separate fixture whose openings are not described by the S1 geometry.
- **Twelve genuine layout defects.** The first live-renderer audit (real bounding boxes) returned 12 findings, including four clipped lines in the S5 separated-stack panel, a clipped C-SPAN line on both ceiling boards, and two mirrored-label collisions on the reflected Ceiling Focus board. All are fixed; the audit is now clean. The earlier pass recorded a zero-clipping sweep, and this pass could not reproduce it: the estimate-based sweep did not see these. A zero-clipping claim is only as good as the measurement behind it.
- **A plan sheet showing a height diagram.** The ceiling-footprint board — whose durable view is *Ceiling footprint* — drew its two regions as stacked horizontal bands, i.e. a section-like height diagram on a plan. It now draws real footprints (7 × 3.22 m and 7 × 3.28 m) with the 0.50 m overlap band, the 1.00 m generated east strip and South's generated closure, so "overhead coverage is not room ownership" is visible rather than asserted.
- **A draft card that contradicted its own narration.** The room-outline draft showed Flat / Suspended / underside Y 3.05 while the board narrated shed / Closure / 3.55–4.55. The draft card now matches the scenario it belongs to, and the room-free draft keeps its own Flat / Suspended reading.
- **A stale instruction.** The wall Inspector told the reader to use a Reveal control on the instrument bar that was no longer there; it now points to the board that owns Reveal.
- **Verification that can rot.** The exported vectors were hand-composed around the atlas's drawings, so a drawing could change while the export went stale. The exporter is now a module, `check-atlas.mjs` compares each committed vector against a fresh render (including the embedded drawing), and a drift is a failure.

## 5. Journeys actually exercised in the live atlas

1. **Place → edit → commit → return → Undo → 3D.** Head 2.10 → 2.30 committed as one transaction; Return to Plan keeps the location and O-DOOR; Undo restores 2.10 with the view unchanged (history 1 → 0); 3D keeps O-DOOR selected.
2. **Depth → excluded selection → Reveal → edit → Undo → Restore view.** Depth 4.50 → 3.00 keeps O-WINDOW selected and outside the drawing with a reason; Reveal admits the window plus its host-wall context; sill 0.40 commits and Undo returns 1.10 while Reveal stays; Restore view clears the reveal and leaves the sill alone.
3. **Curved elevation → change the wall → resume on the changed wall.** The tangent is taken at the wall's *current* canonical station. This wall carries no anchored opening, so that station is its **arc-length midpoint**: after the bend it moves with the wall — 2.06 m on the 4.13 m canonical curve → 1.87 m on the 3.75 m bent curve, with the projected image following (2.80 → 2.56 m). That is a derived midpoint, **not** retention of one fixed absolute arc-length station. An elevation anchored to a selected opening instead follows that opening's canonical host location. A separately authored *fixed* station would need its own explicit policy for geometry change, wall shortening and out-of-range recovery; REGISTER does not propose one, and the prototype adds no station persistence to imply otherwise. Locate in Plan keeps W-CURVE; revisiting the board restores the canonical before-state rather than the previous visitor's toggle.

## 6. Prototype limits — stated plainly

**The visual acceptance gate ran, with one method caveat.** All six specimens were inspected at 1:1 (§1 layer 5). Capturing was intermittent: the panel returned “no frames … webview is not being composited” for long stretches, including for a fresh tab and for a directly opened exported vector, and immediately after a state change it returned the *previous* paint. Every reading above was therefore taken from a follow-up capture of the state under review and cross-checked against the DOM (`frameHtml()`) and the checker's disclosed-geometry assertions, rather than trusted from a single image. What has **not** been inspected visually: the 23 remaining boards, the scaled-viewport appearance beyond the measurements in §1 layer 5, contrast, and any screen-reader behaviour. A design drawing is also not pixel-diffed against the atlas, so a subtle drift between the exports and the pages would not be caught by eye.

The atlas is a scenario player with a small working opening-edit history, a working depth/crop/reveal lease, and a working room-outline ceiling creation. It is **not** a geometry implementation. It does not execute the canonical compiler, persist a project, implement arbitrary pointer dragging, or simulate occlusion. Specifically:

- Blue solid grips are wired; dashed grips are illustrated drags (`aria-label` says so) and change nothing but the status line. Illustrated: range endpoints and look arrow, depth edge, eave / ridge / rise / station grips, whole-underside lift, platform top, and the plan-inset bend handle. `check-atlas.mjs` asserts both halves of that rule — an illustrated handle may not edit the document, and it may not share an action with a live command — so the bend demonstration has exactly one owner, the Inspector.
- **The Navigator is a legend, not a selector.** Its rows carry the canonical names, references and the current selection, and its search filter works, but clicking a row does not select anything in the prototype. The scenario board or an Inspector action is the route to a source; nothing in Plan is hit-testable.
- **Committing** is real only for opening edits and the room-outline ceiling creation. The room-free draft's Create, the joint trim, the bend, the profile conversions and the arch rise describe designed transactions and are annotated as such; they do not add regions or alter canonical geometry.
- Deletion recovery is the global Undo only; the prototype does not model a real deletion transaction list.
- Board changes swap fixtures and load the relevant target; they do not carry live state between drawings, except where a journey above says state survives a *view* change.
- The Section B and Section C registration-map locations are illustrative; the S4 wing is a separate fixture from the two-gallery S1 scene; ceiling regions are not persisted between boards.

### 7.2 Owner rulings recorded 2026-09-23

The four items this section previously listed as open were put to the owner and answered. Each is recorded with its disposition, what it affirms, and what it deliberately does **not** settle. None of them ratifies REGISTER as the P26 direction, selects it over another submission, approves its whole interaction model, or authorizes implementation or a child plan; R1 affirms a modeling ambition whose final interaction model and detailed physical semantics remain subject to A/B/REGISTER synthesis.

**R1 — Independent CeilingRegions: AFFIRMED.**
P26's architectural direction is independent, Layout-owned ceiling regions. A ceiling does not have to belong to a room or inherit its footprint. The affirmed vocabulary: room-seeded and multi-room regions; independently drawn, room-free and partial footprints; several regions inside one room; regions overlapping horizontally at different elevations; and explicit treatment of real solid intersections. **Closure** and **Suspended** are accepted candidate relationships for synthesis — Closure replaces generated overhead only within its footprint, so uncovered portions keep generated closure; Suspended coexists with overhead above it; vertically separated solids may overlap in footprint without a joint; and a genuine solid intersection must never be resolved silently by draw order or automatic union. Directional ceiling trimming stays part of the desired capability subject to geometry feasibility and progressive delivery; the atlas therefore keeps its Trim demonstration labelled as a proposal, and the full joint mechanism is **not** presented as an approved implementation contract (direction §5, §12).

**R2 — Independently authored opening arch rise: APPROVED.**
Arch rise becomes a P26 architectural capability independent of opening width; the current semicircular rise derived from width is insufficient for the intended opening vocabulary. Requirements carried into implementation planning: legacy documents must keep their current appearance when read; **head** remains the opening's overall top; editing rise moves the spring line while holding head fixed unless the creator explicitly chooses another operation; and the property belongs to the canonical architectural model, shared validation and the compiler — not to an Inspector-only or renderer-only override. The exact representation, profile compatibility and migration mechanics are implementation-planning work. The atlas's S3 rise demonstration stays labelled as not-shipped behaviour.

**R3 — S4 finite-depth semantics: CONFIRMED.**
The canonical twelve-room scenario stays in the designer brief. The north–south cut looks east over a finite 6 m range: column 2 is intersected, column 3 supplies projected room context, column 1 is behind the cut, column 4 is beyond depth — six room contexts included, six excluded. Including a room context does not mean every geometric primitive of that room is inside the finite range; individual geometry still obeys the actual membership and clipping boundary, which is why Reveal admits one source and its host context rather than a whole column. No alternative section-box or named-range model is introduced to explain this fixture; broader section-volume interaction choices remain available for comparison during final synthesis.

**R4 — Multiple fixtures in one project: CLOSED AS NON-DECISION.**
S1, S4 and the other canonical scenarios are independent design and test fixtures. Their appearance in one atlas does **not** establish that one Museum Editor project must contain several wings, architectural documents, floors or separate scenes. Fixture transitions stay explicit in the atlas so a reviewer cannot mistake them for movement inside one canonical project, and P26's document or Navigator architecture is not expanded to accommodate the atlas's presentation structure. Large-building organization is separate product work.

### 7.3 Carried forward to synthesis (deliberately not ratified here)

1. **Ceiling datum under a floor change.** REGISTER proposes an authored absolute underside Y with floor-relative readouts and a floor edit that does not silently translate independently authored ceilings (direction §11.4). What an independent ceiling spanning several rooms should do when the floor datum changes is unresolved, and the prototype must not decide it by accident.

Today's maximum-boundary-wall derivation is not assumed to be the finished enclosed-room experience.

These two questions are important to the final product direction and do not block this submission as an independent proposal.
