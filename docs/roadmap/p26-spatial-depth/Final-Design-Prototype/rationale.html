Museum Editor · P26 design synthesis of B and D

One museum. You stand, you open, it settles.

Plan, 3D, Section, Wall face and Looking up stop being places you switch between. They become standpoints of one camera around one model you can temporarily open — by a verb or by hand. Whenever the camera lines up with a wall, a cut or the sky, the picture settles to scale: perspective drains away and the green mat becomes drafting paper. Editing is not a mode you reach: a handle exists wherever its axis reads, in Plan, in 3D, on a curve at any wrap.

The unified interaction model

Two moves, one promise — and edit wherever you can read it.

Every capability in P26 — wall tops, openings, curved walls, ceilings, sections, precise numbers — is reached by standing somewhere and opening what is in the way. Nothing is a separate workspace, and neither move changes the building. The picture settles when it is to scale. The building changes only when you edit a number, which you can do wherever its handle's axis reads.

STAND

Move the one camera

There is one camera and it always moves continuously. Plan is that camera tipped straight down, with a horizontal cut sliding to 1.20. Facing a wall, looking through a cut, and looking up are the same camera arriving somewhere else.

  • 1/2 or the tilt bead between Plan and 3D
  • F face the selection · double-click a wall
OPEN

Displace the architecture, briefly

What hides the subject gets out of the way physically, and always goes back. A curved wall unrolls — pull its corner and stop anywhere. A ceiling lifts by its tab. The museum parts along a line you draw and slide. Walls in front are set aside. The as-built position stays drawn, dashed, the whole time.

  • O opens the selection in one beat · drag its corner or tab for any amount
  • Esc steps back one level · ⇧Esc puts everything back
SETTLE

The picture says when it is to scale

Near a principal direction the camera has a detent. As it falls in, a dolly-zoom takes perspective to zero and the mat turns to paper. Settle is a promise about the picture, per axis: a round wall seen square is to scale in height only, so it gets a vertical scale bar and no horizontal one. It gates nothing — handles follow the legibility rule below, and the numbers are always measured on the model.

  • Release within 14° and it settles for you · S squares up
  • Drag away and perspective returns gradually
One selection, everywhere.Navigator, drawing, Inspector and status all name the same entity through every move. If it leaves the view, it stays selected and says why.
View trail ≠ Undo.Standpoints go on a trail you can walk back along with [ — including reopening a cut you closed. Undo holds building changes only, and never moves the camera.
Nesting is not history.Opening a different kind of view from inside one nests it; the strip shows the path (3D › Section › Facing). Esc steps back one crumb, exactly as you left it; ⇧Esc or the first crumb closes everything. The trail ([ ]) is time, not structure; Undo is the building.
Handles where the axis reads.A handle is offered wherever its own axis is legible on screen and withheld where it runs along your line of sight — heights from above, widths at a curve's silhouette. The number stays typeable. No view has its own handle list.
View state looks like view state.A displaced wall wears paper; the as-built position stays dashed; the strip says “view only”. Coming back, the editor names the building changes you made while it was open — they stay, with one button to undo them.
One colour, one meaning.PLATE blue is selection and nothing else. Yellow tape is a number you can type. Dashed slate is displacement, coral a problem, green open on purpose.
One writer per number.Handles, typed tapes and Inspector fields are the same value. Invalid proposals are refused with a reason, and a refused gesture writes nothing.
Motion that learns.A move plays slowly with a caption the first two times you see it, then gets brisk. Shift makes any move instant. Reduced-motion is respected.
Curvature is a view.A curved wall can be held anywhere from its round 360° to flat, from inside or outside, and edited at every stop. Unrolling never stretches the wall, so the numbers are always measured; only the picture foreshortens — and it says so, per axis.

What came from where

Not B plus D. A model neither proposal had, built from the strongest parts of both.

B's insight was that continuity comes from the camera: if you watch yourself walk round to a view, you never lose the room. D's insight was that the architecture itself can move out of the way: unrolled, lifted, parted — by hand. Together they needed a third idea — when is a moving view exact? The answer turned out to be two answers: the picture is exact when it settles, per axis; an edit is exact wherever its handle's axis reads. A peer review exposed that the first version had merged them, and had lost several of B's and D's best moves on the way; see the reconciliation.

Retained from B

Standpoint and continuity

  • Continuous camera movement between every standpoint, never a jump cut.
  • “Plan is a blade pointing down; looking up is a blade pointing up” — kept as the literal mechanism (horizontal cut at 1.20 / 1.60), not the vocabulary.
  • Live preview before commit: the cut previews in place in 3D, and as a peek in Plan.
  • Explicit look direction and depth, with depth framed as “how far in to include”, and a view change, not an edit.
  • Excluded ≠ gone, with B's recovery: include it (depth to exactly the number needed), go to its wall and come straight back, and a ghost place on the paper.
  • Exploratory preview: slide the drawn line and watch the cut re-derive before committing.
  • Exact return: back restores the standpoint, depth and Reveal you left.
  • Selection travels; “work on its wall” from an opening.
  • View history separate from Undo.
  • Honesty about curved walls: the picture is foreshortened, and the product says so. The rule B drew from it — no width edits face-on — is replaced below.
Retained from D

The physical model and the look

  • The visual language: green mat, foam model, yellow measuring tape for numbers, Bricolage / Onest / Martian Mono.
  • Direct physical opening: pull a wall's corner, lift a lid by its tab.
  • Inside / Outside for a wall you face — now a walk round, not a toggle.
  • Find anything, and say why it can't be seen from here.
  • The session summary on leaving: what you changed, and undo them together if you choose.
  • One verb, meaning set by the selection: O unfolds a wall, lifts a ceiling, or with nothing selected draws a line.
  • Unfold around the clicked point, seam S, junction letters A/B, both-side ceilings drawn on the sheet.
  • Lift with tethers: meets / gap / open on purpose, and fixes previewed on hover.
  • Open along a drawn line, the near half sliding toward you — the central Section metaphor.
  • Set aside, never silently removed; states named in the Navigator (“laid flat”, “opened away”).
  • Every number is a tape; a person figure for scale.
New in this synthesis

Invented

  • Settle: a detent that dolly-zooms the picture to scale and turns the mat to paper — per axis, with a vertical-only scale bar on a curve. Flatness is derived from where the camera points, never a mode flag.
  • The legibility gate: one rule decides which handles exist in every view — offered where the axis reads, withheld where it runs along the line of sight.
  • One opening kit for Plan, 3D and a face: authoring an opening is the same act from every standpoint.
  • Nesting with a breadcrumb: Esc steps back one open state, exactly; ⇧Esc puts everything back.
  • One resolver for “where is it?” behind Find, the Inspector, the beacon and the section locator.
  • As-built ghost and label budget: displacement always shows its home; only the selection speaks at full strength.
  • The tilt bead: Plan and 3D shown as two ends of one camera; drag between them.
  • Registration during unfold: the camera rises so the footprint is visible while the wall peels, then settles face-on.
  • Curvature as an editable view: a dial from 360° round to flat, with stops at 180° and 90°. Every stop is editable, square-on or from a 3D standpoint, because the numbers are measured along the wall rather than on the screen.
  • Chained open states: hop between walls without going home; different kinds nest.
  • A trail of recipes: view history that can reopen a closed cut or unfolded wall.
  • Motion that learns, teaching captions only while teaching, Shift for instant.
  • Look up by sinking below the floor, with a mirror toggle rather than a hidden mirror convention.
  • A depth edge you can drag on the “where you are” card.
Changed or rejected

What did not survive

  • B's all-orthographic painter camera → rejected. Perspective is how people read space; orthographic is a destination the camera settles into, not the medium.
  • B's blade as the user-facing object → replaced by D's drawn line. The blade survives underneath as the plan and look-up cuts.
  • B's “no width edits on a foreshortened curve” → replaced. Handles measure distance along the wall surface, which unrolling preserves exactly, so they are true at any curvature and any camera angle.
  • B's height crop and segmented-panel curves → deferred; the model reserves them.
  • D's floating session bar → moved into PLATE's contextual strip under Plan · 3D, as a breadcrumb.
  • D's full-bleed Paper → not recovered: PLATE's Navigator and Inspector stay (the Inspector is the one writer). The locator now appears only while something is open.
  • D's fold map + B's where-you-are → merged into one live locator (camera, anchor, junctions, cut, depth).
  • D's peel-by-hand corner → first dropped as a duplicate of the verb, then restored: once curvature is a view, the peel is the only gesture that sets any curvature from where you stand.
  • D's “one undo for the whole session” → reinterpreted. One gesture, one Undo step; on leaving, a summary offers to undo that session's steps together.
  • “Esc puts everything back” → reversed after review. It skipped the section you came from; Esc is now one level.
  • B's hover-before-drawing → reinterpreted as draw-then-slide; a free-angle line needs its direction first.
  • D's ask-every-time “follow or stay” → not yet; relationships are shown instead (see open questions).
Against the P26 brief

How it lands

  • Instruments stay contextual: the strip sits under Plan · 3D, never beside them, with one return action.
  • Finite depth by default (6.0 m), exclusion with reasons, Reveal as a view-only exception.
  • Openings resolve through their host: outlines and handles come from the host relation, never from wall faces.
  • Wall-top profiles (level · slope · gable, by distance along the wall, including the curve), independent arch rise with head held (owner ruling R2), ceiling regions that close or hang (R1).
  • Floor datum +0.15 is shown in the Navigator, on every drawing, and in world readouts.
  • Scene artwork is passive context in every drawing; edit it in Arrange.

Technical and architectural questions to investigate later

What this depends on.

Plan in the same rendererToday's Plan is its own drafting surface. Do plan tools move into a WebGL orthographic view, or does the SVG plan crossfade in at the moment of settle? Line weight, text and hit-testing precision decide it.
“One nav + one motion”AGENTS.md reserves camera motion to camera-route/camera-motion. The editor viewport choreography here must be confirmed as editor-only viewport animation, not a second visitor motion system — or be built on that core.
Caps from compiled solidsThe prototype computes section and plan caps analytically from centrelines. Production needs caps from the one compiler's solids, with per-source identity, mitred junctions, cubic curves, sloped tops and holed slabs.
Membership evaluated once (I11)Here, clipping planes draw the cut while memberOf() separately answers the Navigator. Production needs one membership stage feeding render, hit, snap and overlays.
Unrolling real wallsP23 curves are cubic, not circular arcs. Unrolling needs arc-length parameterisation, a seam policy for closed loops, and a GPU bend deformation rather than re-tessellating every frame.
Displacement as view stateWhere does a “lifted” ceiling or a “parted” half live: render transforms per source outside the document, with hosted objects following? Visitor isolation must be guaranteed.
True orthographic vs tiny field of viewThe prototype approximates orthographic with a sub-degree perspective. A matched OrthographicCamera swap may be more robust; handle maths must use exactly the rendered projection either way.
Ceiling semanticsClosure / suspended are shown; generated-closure fallback, partial coverage strips, overlap joints and spanning regions are not prototyped. Datum policy for ceilings under a floor change is still open.
Who owns a height?When a wall rises past its ceiling, or a ceiling tilts, does anything follow? The prototype only shows relationships. An acyclic “fit to” rule is a product decision.
Trail as recipesShould standpoints and cut definitions survive the session (pinned views)? They must store the instrument definition, never derived geometry.
Performance under P23BEvery open state rebuilds some geometry per frame here. Real models need GPU-side deformation and cached derivation; nothing here assumes P23B has landed.
Paper width inside PLATEAt 1440 the work column gives the museum 816 px against D's 1204. Should PLATE sanction a Navigator collapse for large drawings, without losing the rank the brief requires?
Legibility thresholdscos 70° and 14 px/m are prototype values. They need tuning with real users and devices, and a rule for touch.
Accessibility of motion and handlesKeyboard-only handle nudging, screen-reader naming of open states, and a reduced-motion path that still explains correspondence (a crossfade with the registration lines).

No user testing has been done. The claims here are design judgements checked against scripted journeys. First things to test: whether “heights to scale” is understood without the caption, whether people find the corner to peel unprompted, and whether Esc-one-level feels right after a long chain. Not yet prototyped: drawing new walls in the one renderer, vertical crop and cut-only, a twelve-room museum.
