## 4. What P23B.2 established about external mechanisms

### 4.1 The five mechanism families, and the two distinctions that matter most

P23B.2 groups the external precedents into five families — shared geometric representations · spatial
broad-phase filtering · dependency-aware recomputation · derived-artifact reuse · execution isolation —
and explicitly labels them "research candidates, not accepted implementation decisions". Two of its
distinctions govern how this synthesis may use them:

```text
X-1  "a spatial index can reduce candidate pairs, but it cannot establish curved-intersection
     correctness by itself."  ⇒ Broad-phase precedent licenses PRUNING, never a weaker predicate.
X-2  "a dirty-node system can limit recomputation, but it does not establish that document-wide
     invariants remain valid."  ⇒ Dependency-tracking precedent licenses SCOPE REDUCTION, never the
     removal of a whole-document obligation.
```

### 4.2 The precedents, by the question they answer

Evidence labels are P23B.2's own: **SV** = source-verified at a pinned revision · **DOC** = documented ·
**INF** = applicability inference. No external performance figure was transferred into a Museum Editor
projection (P23B.2 §F), and P23B.3 adds none.

| Question | Precedent (label) | What it actually shows | The research's own limit on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 shared representation | OpenPlan3D shared faceted path, `roomDetection.ts`/`wallPathSpans` retaining source Wall ids (**SV**); Bezier.js `reduce`/`curveintersects`/`pairiteration` (**SV**); IfcOpenShell geometry iterator (**DOC**) | one derived curve representation can serve several consumers; reusable curve-processing structure exists | Bezier.js "demonstrates reusable curve-processing structure, not a verified equivalent of Museum Editor's sampling semantics"; IfcOpenShell's cache key was **not** established |

### 4.3 The research finding that most changes this synthesis

P23B.2 states the invalid-baseline rule directly, and it is the same conclusion §5/T-2 and §8.6 reached
from internal evidence alone:

```text
"If an exhaustive baseline is known valid, an incremental gate can investigate changed geometry against
 the relevant unchanged geometry, subject to complete dependency tracking.
 If the baseline contains unresolved invalid pairs, checking only newly changed pairs cannot establish
 whole-document validity. The existing violations still exist.
 Therefore, any affected-subset proof must specify its baseline-validity precondition or preserve the
 pre-existing issue set. The product decision about what edits to permit remains open."
```

That is an external, source-backed confirmation that **M-2b verdict reuse is conditional on the
curved-crossing ruling** (§9.1) rather than merely a design preference — and it is why abandonment
condition AB-3 is not an implementer's judgement call.

### 4.4 The rules this synthesis adopts for using precedent

```text
U-1  PRECEDENT LICENSES A QUESTION, NOT A MECHANISM. A cited project shows that a shape is solvable and
     what its failure modes are. Our mechanism is admitted only by internal evidence plus an equivalence
     proof answerable from our own code (§8.4).
U-2  NO FEATURE REQUIREMENT IS DERIVED FROM PRECEDENT. Every row in §6 states its motivation from OUR
     evidence IDs; the precedent column is supporting context, never the reason.
U-3  LABELS TRAVEL. Every external claim keeps P23B.2's SV/DOC/INF label and its pinned source in
     P23B.2 §F. A claim about an architecture we cannot inspect may not become a constraint on us.
U-4  THE RESEARCH'S OWN LIMITS OUTRANK ITS OPTIMISM. Where P23B.2 says an R-tree's economics are
     unproven at our sizes, or that the JSON check is too cheap to replace, or that naive per-Wall id
     reuse is unsafe, the mechanism ledger follows the limit, not the mechanism family.
U-5  P23B.2 IS UNREVIEWED EVIDENCE. It is used here as research input; its acceptance is the owner's,
     like P23B.1's. Nothing in §7 depends on it being formally accepted — and nothing in §7 required it
     to exist (§8.6 AB-8 still governs: a mechanism whose ONLY justification is a precedent we could
     not establish returns to `unresolved`).
```

### T-1 Repeated curve sampling across topology pre/post, face extraction and compilation

```text
Question  Are the existing consumers' sampling configurations equivalent, and is a shared sampled
          representation therefore justified?
Evidence  P-2 (five consumers, ≈280 operations/release on E3), S-1 (all options equivalent by
          construction — one adapter, no override anywhere), S-2 (traversal is the only divergence and
          reverse is re-derived), EQ-1…EQ-5 (proof obligations).
VERIFIED CURRENT BEHAVIOR
  · one sampler seam, uniform sampling options, no shared representation;
  · the same (document, Wall) centerline is re-derived independently 4x per release on E3;

    negative-area outer ring.
CONCLUSION — the blocker to sharing is NOT sampling configuration (S-1 closed it). It is exactly three
  things, all provable:
    (a) TRAVERSAL — a key must include it, or a reversed read must be proven equal to an independent

        key must be built from values the code can actually compare;
    (c) ARRAY EQUALITY on hit vs miss, not geometric near-equality (EQ-3).
CLASSIFICATION  proposed direction — a per-Wall sampled-centerline derivation, keyed on
  (canonical centerline identity, traversal, resolved endpoints), consumed by topology-pre/post, face
  extraction, the Opening set and the compile, is JUSTIFIED and is the single highest-value structural
  change available. It is not justified as a "global sample cache" and must not become a second
  geometry truth (see §6, M-1 row).
DO NOT  propose one cache for all consumers before (a)–(c) are tested; do not sum the 280 operations
  into a predicted saving (M-3); do not touch `sampleSegment`'s default options — changing them
  changes compiled geometry and is a product change, not an optimization.
```

### T-2 Whole-document preflight versus affected-subset validation

```text
Question  Can the whole-document validation that runs per pointer move and twice per release be
          scoped, given a genuinely document-wide invariant — including cross-Room crossings — and
          given that baseline documents may already be invalid?
Evidence  P-5 (whole-document preflight per move), P-6 (pre/post pair per release), P-10 (four divergent
          scopes; the crossing gate is all-pairs by design), F-C3 (the counterexample: a Room duplicate
          that translates a curved Wall into a crossing with a Wall OUTSIDE its own Room), EQ-7.
VERIFIED CURRENT BEHAVIOR
  · the crossing rule is all-pairs and carries no notion of the edited Wall;
  · Room-local reasoning is provably insufficient — F-C3 is a case where locality misses the defect.
CONCLUSION  Locality is the WRONG basis for scoping. Sound scoping must be an EXTENT argument: after an
  edit, which Wall pairs can have changed relationship? A pair whose swept extents cannot overlap
  cannot newly cross, and a pair whose verdict has not changed may reuse its recorded verdict. This
  makes scoping a dependency/verdict-reuse problem (P23B.4), not a "shrink the loop bound" problem.
CLASSIFICATION
  proposed direction — extent/AABB-derived pair pruning plus reuse of UNCHANGED pair verdicts for the
    pre/post pair (P-6), because the post gate differs from the pre gate only by what reconciliation
    changed;
  unresolved — whether "reuse the unchanged remainder's verdict" is admissible depends on the
    curved-crossing ruling (§9.1): an admitted-invalid document (PO-1 option B/D) makes a *cached
    negative verdict* part of the product's semantics rather than a pure optimization. P23B.4 must not
    implement verdict reuse before that ruling.
MUST NOT  present any scoped validation as equivalent until the differential test in §8.4 proves
  full-vs-scoped equality on the E1/E3 fixtures AND on a fixture edited into an inter-Room crossing.
EXTERNAL PRECEDENT (context, not authority)  FreeCAD's touched/`mustRecompute` + `Document::recompute`
  (**SV**) and Pascal's dirty-entity propagation (**SV**) show the dependency-tracking SHAPE; RBush /
  Flatbush / CGAL (**SV**) show the pair-pruning shape. The research's own limit binds harder than its
  precedent: **no evidence establishes an R-tree as the faster choice at our document sizes**, and
  "box overlap alone proves no intersection" — so M-2a ships as a plain extent scan first (§6.2).
EXTERNALLY CORROBORATED RULING DEPENDENCY  P23B.2 §Q3 reaches the invalid-baseline precondition
  independently: checking only newly changed pairs "cannot establish whole-document validity" when the
  baseline already contains unresolved invalid pairs (§4.3, §6.5 O-3).
OPTION E CHANGES THE INVARIANT'S SUBJECT, NOT ITS STRENGTH (added — decision record §2.10)
  The owner-directed direction makes geometric intersection between INDEPENDENT groups permitted
  geometry, so the whole-document invariant T-2 reasons about becomes a GROUP-SCOPED invariant. Two
  consequences this synthesis carries:
    · the EXTENT ARGUMENT SURVIVES UNCHANGED. It is a claim about which Wall PAIRS can have changed
      relationship, which is orthogonal to what the rule's subject is;
    · the ORACLE MOVES. Full-vs-scoped equality must be asserted against the validator that ACTUALLY
      SHIPS. Under Path 2 that is today's document-global gate, where an independent-group crossing is
      STILL a rejection — so a pruning that stopped reporting it would be a regression, not an
      improvement. M-2a must therefore treat a permitted independent overlap exactly as the predicate
      it wraps does, and must not be re-scoped to a group model that does not exist yet (§6.7).
```

### 6.6 NEW correctness obligation for Worker isolation (→ P23B.8)

P23B.2 §Q8 identifies a hazard this synthesis did not previously state, and it is a correctness
obligation rather than a performance question:

```text
Baseline B  →  Candidate A starts  →  Candidate C supersedes A  →  Result A arrives
                                                                    ↓
                                        A must NOT replace C, must not install stale geometry,
                                        must not create a history entry, must not alter selection
```

Consequences folded into P23B.8: a Worker (or ANY asynchronous acceptance path) requires (a) a
**document revision / candidate transaction identity** carried with every result, (b) **superseded-result
rejection** on arrival, (c) an explicit **cancellation** decision — ignoring an obsolete result prevents
stale installation but does NOT stop its CPU work, while `Worker.terminate()` stops the work but discards
reusable Worker state — and (d) **overlapping-gesture and Worker-failure tests**. These requirements are
independent of which Worker library, if any, is ever chosen, so they belong in P23B.8's decision record
even if the answer is "not justified". They also raise the bar for admitting async acceptance: it adds a
new ordering invariant to a pipeline whose determinism the phase is required to preserve.

## 7. The optimization direction

### 7.1 The direction, in one paragraph

The measured hitch is a **synchronous release/acceptance block** whose dominant tagged component is the
canonical compile, and whose most redundant component is that the **same Wall centerline is re-derived
independently by four consumers per release**. The direction is therefore: (1) complete the durable
measurement foundation so any claim is reproducible; then (2) **derive each Wall's samples once per
release behind the existing seam** and let topology, face extraction, the Opening set and the compile
consume one representation, keyed on centerline identity so the same key serves pre/post reuse and
baseline→candidate reuse; then (3) **scope validation by extent rather than locality** and reuse the
unchanged remainder of the pre/post pair; then (4) **reuse unchanged per-Wall/per-Room artifacts across
a candidate** with the identity⇒equality discipline the existing WeakMaps already demonstrate; then
(5) take the missing **presentation measurements** and only then touch rendering; then (6) re-measure
interaction and decide **honestly** whether anything remains that a Worker, or Rust/WASM, would fix.
Every step is equivalence-preserving, and each one's first test is an equivalence test, not a timing
test.

