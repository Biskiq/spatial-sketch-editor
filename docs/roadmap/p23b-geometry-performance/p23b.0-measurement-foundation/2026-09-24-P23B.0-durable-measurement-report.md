## 0. The finding

**The editor is slow on every fixture, including the one with no curvature at all.**

Measured in one browser session, on the same machine, with the three hosted fixtures driven through the
same fixed targets at one shared `19.292586 px/m` viewport (§4):

The flush and frame rows above are observed press/move/wheel phases — see §0.2, which is why they are quoted as phase evidence and not as
end-to-end settle time.

### 0.1 What this establishes

- **Curvature is not necessary for sluggishness.** The straight control has synchronous accepted
  authoring/release medians of 262.6/208.4 ms and selection input of 121.7 ms.
- **Curvature amplifies several observed costs.** The controlled straight/all-curved comparison shows
  higher synchronous and press/move-phase deferred costs. Ratios alone do not establish which mechanism
  dominates, and these measurements do not establish one root cause across all paths.
- **Plan updates are a justified investigation target.** Pan/zoom's captured flush median rises from
  22.4 to 343.2 ms without a canonical edit-planning call. This is flush latency, not painted-frame time,
  and does not establish that every editing delay lies outside the planner.

### 0.2 Coverage limit of the preserved capture (stated, not hidden)

This capture was taken before the post-release scheduling correction in §10.1. At that revision, an action
whose press pair had already settled completed the moment its outcome was resolved, which refused the
schedule call that follows every release and authoring click. So:

- the **flush and frame samples are phase evidence, not complete action-settlement evidence**.
  Post-release pairs are missing when the preceding pair settled before resolution, including selection
  and pan pointer releases as well as drag/edit, bend and authoring clicks;
- **zero dropped boundaries does not prove complete coverage.** The dropped counter only sees a scheduled
  callback that outlives its session; a pair that was never scheduled is invisible to it. That is exactly
  how this gap survived the previous evidence gates;
- the recorded selection press and pan/zoom press/move/wheel pairs remain useful as those specific
  phases; they do not establish complete selection/pan release coverage. Every **synchronous** boundary
  — `input`, `release`, `reactive`, `plan-apply` — is unaffected, because
  it is measured inside the handler before any resolution.

What the preserved baseline therefore still supports: the accepted-release latencies (§6.2), the pointer
input distributions, the planner and reactive costs, the pan/zoom and selection flush/frame phases, the
same-size straight/all-curved comparison, the separate owner workload, and the capture's sampling evidence
(warm-ups, completions, retries, resets, settled sessions, isolated sessions). What it does **not**
support: any claim about flush or frame time *after* an accepted release or commit, and therefore any
end-to-end "time until settled" claim for edits. §10.1 records the fix; §10.3 records the recommended owner disposition.

### 0.3 Where to look first (not a diagnosis)

The captured pan/zoom flush and the straight control's synchronous delays justify inspecting Plan update
work alongside release-path planning. Use the existing nested marks and source traces to identify a
specific mechanism before selecting an optimization. Do not redirect P23B.4 into rendering work without
reconciling its approved scope and the later rendering/interaction slices.

Reading the table:

- The accepted distributions contain **only accepted actions**; the authoring path's setup clicks are
  recorded as `setup` counts and stay outside them (§6.2). The warm-up window is excluded per *action* on
  the path, so the authoring release distribution is 23 measured commits out of 25 (§5 above).
- The warm-up exclusion is 5 for every applicable path and is applied in code, not in prose.
- A retry count is recorded per path. On the authoring path it equals the setup-click count (25, 25, 25):
  the retry rule is "an accepted action that follows a non-accepted attempt", and the authoring protocol
  is by definition a non-committing setup click followed by its commit. That is not a failed attempt; it
  is the shape of the path, and it is why the field is reported beside the outcome tally instead of being

- `unclassified` actions are hovers: a pointer move with no press behind it is its own completed action

- The straight control records zero bend samples and the deferred 3D path records zero samples on all

### 6.3 Attribution correction

The v4 record filed the canonical planner/apply call under `adapter`, then concluded the cost was "outside
the canonical planner". v5 separates the two, and the numbers confirm the correction: on the owner case the
v4 `adapter` bucket read p50 49.5 ms / 56.6 ms and the v5 `plan-apply` boundary reads **49.8 ms / 53.5 ms**
— the same work, correctly named. The Three adapter boundary is **unavailable on every 2D path** (it is
emitted by the 3D scene path only) and is recorded with that reason rather than being filled with planner
time.

### 6.4 Nesting, stated once

`input`, `release`, `plan-apply`, `adapter` and `reactive` are synchronous `[call start, call end]`
boundaries. `svelte-flush` and `browser-frame` are deferred and start where the synchronous input **ended**,
so a frame encloses its own flush and is never additive with it. 38 existing P23.11 marks are listed in
`nestedMarks` beside the P23B boundaries and must not be summed into them. The corrected origin rule is visible in the data: flush p50 ≈ frame p50 on every path (e.g. straight selection 15.8 / 17.1 ms, all-curved
selection 245.1 / 246.6 ms), where v4 had pooled two different origins under one metric.

One further rule is part of this contract and was **missing from the capture revision**: an action may
complete only after its own last `input`/`release` boundary has had that deferred pair scheduled, so a
release cannot resolve the outcome and complete the action before its post-release flush/frame exists. That
is §10.1, and it is why the drag/edit, bend and authoring flush/frame figures above are phase evidence
rather than end-to-end settle time (§0.2).

### 10.1 Second correction round — post-release boundary scheduling

**Defect.** Every release and authoring-click call site resolves the action's outcome first and schedules
that boundary's deferred flush/frame second. `completeIfSettled` only refused completion while `pending`
was above zero, and a press whose pair had already settled leaves `pending` at zero. The action therefore
completed on the resolution, the schedule call that followed was refused as not-writable, and the pair
measured from the **end of the release or click handler was never scheduled**. Nothing was counted as
dropped — `droppedBoundaries` only sees a callback that was scheduled and then outlived its session — which
is how the gap survived the previous evidence gates (§0.2).

**Fix.** An action now also requires that its own last `input`/`release` boundary has had a pair scheduled:
scheduling records `scheduledThrough = syncCount`, `completeIfSettled` refuses while
`scheduledThrough < syncCount`, and ambient boundaries (`reactive`, `plan-apply`, `adapter`) are
