## 1. Problem, and the evidence that motivates it

```text
1.1  ONE RULE, FOUR SCOPES, AND THEY DISAGREE (F-C7, F-C1, F-C6). The same crossing rule is applied with
     four different subjects, so an operation can ADMIT what a later gate REJECTS:
       Room duplicate      isolation → codec → validateBatchWallTopology (chord-exact)   curve crossing NOT checked
       Room move           codec → validateWallFirstTopology (full) → portals            checked, REJECTS
       Wall chain authoring auto-adopt/auto-node → validateChainTopology (full)          checked, rejects the curved case
       import              codec only                                                    NOT checked
       compile             per-Room boundary geometry only                               BLIND (F-C2)
     The consequence is the reported defect: a duplicate or an import admits a curved inter-Room crossing,
     and the NEXT unrelated edit is then rejected and blamed for pre-existing geometry.

1.2  THE OPERATOR'S DIRECTION CHANGES THE SUBJECT, NOT THE RULE (D-1, §2.10). Under Option E an
     intersection between entities that are NOT in the same connected component is intentionally
     permitted geometry. The rule stays one rule; its subject becomes component-scoped.

1.3  CONNECTIVITY IS ALREADY DERIVED, AND JUNCTION IDENTITY IS ITS AUTHORITY (§2.11 Q1/Q3). A group is
     the transitive closure over shared boundary Junction ids. Portal relations are DEPENDENT — validated
     against derived adjacency, never constitutive of it.

1.4  THE COINCIDENCE RULE IS THE HARD PART, AND IT IS NOT A SCHEMA GAP (D-9, §2.11.2). Today
     `duplicate_junction_point` rejects ANY two Junctions within 1e-9 ANYWHERE in the document, and every
     authoring path that resolves a coincident endpoint ADOPTS the existing Junction. Both guards act
     document-wide. Two of the owner's requirements — duplicate a Room at exactly the same coordinates,
     and independent Walls with identical endpoint coordinates — land directly on them.

1.5  THE AUDIT WAS INCOMPLETE IN TWO WAYS (§2.11 Q7, §2.11.3), and both are THIS slice's obligations:
       GAP 1  `connectedRoomIds` is ROOM-ONLY. A standalone Wall, a partition stub or an unenclosed chain
              contributes nothing to it, so the thing Option E must classify cannot be classified by the
              helper the plan originally assumed.
       GAP 2  face-extraction correspondence is GEOMETRIC. `buildCorrespondenceComponents` unions a
              predecessor Room with a candidate face on interior containment OR shared interior area, and
              `polygonsShareInteriorArea`'s adjacency guard is tolerance-based with an explicit
              identical/nested-ring fallback. Coincident or contained graph-INDEPENDENT Rooms therefore
              already collapse into ONE correspondence component.

1.6  UN-8 is closed by this slice's existence: whether the chord-only duplicate gate was a deliberate
     P23.4 scope choice or an omission is answered by re-scoping it, and the misleading comment at
     `layout-duplicate.ts:196-200` is corrected HERE (D-8), alongside the behaviour it describes.
```

## 4. Proposed mechanism, and the alternatives

```text
M-3a-1  GENERAL CONNECTIVITY TEST (new, in `layout-core`). A component label over the Wall graph keyed on
        JUNCTION IDENTITY — never on coordinates — covering Walls that no Room boundary references.
        Room grouping is DERIVED from it, so `connectedRoomIds` keeps its exact current behaviour for
        Room move while the general test serves the gates. ALTERNATIVE: generalise `connectedRoomIds`
        in place. REJECTED as the default because Room move depends on the current output ORDER and
        scope, and changing the helper to serve two consumers invites a regression in the one that works.
M-3a-2  COMPONENT-SCOPED GATE SUBJECT. Each gate filters candidate pairs to same-component before
        applying the UNCHANGED predicate. One predicate, one verdict vocabulary, four consistent scopes.
M-3a-3  COMPONENT-SCOPED COINCIDENCE. `duplicate_junction_point` is evaluated WITHIN a component.
        WHERE that scoping lives — a scoped form of the existing predicate, or a distinct gate — is an
        implementation choice, not an owner decision (D-9).
M-3a-4  AUTHORING INTENT SPLIT. Adoption/crossing-split is retained for an operation EXTENDING a group
        and withdrawn for INDEPENDENT placement. The discriminator is the operation's declared intent,
        not the geometry. ALTERNATIVE: remove adoption entirely. REJECTED — it breaks T13 and the
        within-group authoring that E's containment rule exists to protect.
M-3a-5  JOIN/CONNECT CONTRACT (PLANNING LEVEL ONLY, D-10). Named interface and invariants: an explicit,
        attributable operation that may adopt/merge Junctions between components; atomic; a history
        entry; no silent geometry change. NO UI, mode or command is designed or implemented here.
```

## 5. Correctness invariants and equivalence proof obligations

```text
INV-1  ONE COMPILER, ONE GEOMETRY TRUTH, one crossing rule. No second pipeline, no second predicate.
INV-2  THE PREDICATE IS UNCHANGED. Only WHICH PAIRS are evaluated changes; the verdict vocabulary and
       per-pair outcome are identical for pairs in the SAME component.
INV-3  NO NEW REPRESENTATION. No group id, cluster relation, parent pointer or persisted field.
INV-4  IDENTITY⇒EQUALITY for anything this slice reuses; every guard can refuse.
INV-5  Plan/3D/visitor parity; visitor/editor isolation; `LayoutDocument`/`SceneDocument` separation;
       room-local transforms; deterministic selection and history — all unchanged.
INV-6  Deliberate, point-addressed splits (`planWallSubdivision`, migration replay) behave EXACTLY as
       before: they are not overlap-driven joins and must not be affected by this policy.

OR-D12-1…6  — THE DEDICATED RECONCILIATION ORACLE (decision record §4.2.2). OWNED HERE; must pass BEFORE
             this slice is accepted; NOT satisfied by OR-3 or OR-9b.
  THE GUARANTEE: an UNRELATED group retains its identity; the OPERATED group retains or changes identity
  only per that operation's EXISTING authorized lineage contract; spatial overlap alone may never cause
  either change.
  OR-D12-1  exact coincidence — the unrelated Room keeps its id/name/boundary refs/object+opening ownership
  OR-D12-2  partial overlap — each candidate face is attributed to its OWN predecessor; no
            `ambiguous_room_correspondence`
  OR-D12-3  full containment — the unrelated Room's id survives; containment is not a lineage event
  OR-D12-4  the OPERATED group may still change, per its own contract — and only it
  OR-D12-5  evaluated against the GENERAL Wall/Junction test, so a standalone Wall or partition stub
            overlapping a Room boundary is covered; `connectedRoomIds` must NOT be substituted
  OR-D12-6  exercised through EVERY reconciliation caller: wall delete · role change · dissolve ·
            migration · Room move · chain

OR-3a  SCOPED SUBJECT ≡ the same verdict the pre-policy gate produced for pairs in the SAME component,
       over curved crossings, touching endpoints, shared Junctions, near-tangent curves, reversed Walls,
       multiple Rooms and degenerate boxes.
OR-3b  A permitted INDEPENDENT-GROUP overlap is NOT reported as a failure — and, symmetrically, a
       same-component failure is NOT dropped.
OR-4a  ATOMICITY on refusal: document, identity, selection, history and preview unchanged.
```

## 8. Regression and resource-lifetime risks

```text
R-1  OVER-RELAXATION. A component scope applied too broadly silently admits a same-component failure.
     Guard: OR-3a's superset direction is asserted BOTH ways, and T6–T8 + T13 pin the containment set.
R-2  IDENTITY DRIFT. A geometric correspondence rule can reassign Room identity on overlap.
     Guard: OR-D12-1…6 before acceptance; a union of two independent Rooms is a FAILURE, not a warning.
R-3  PARTIAL RE-SCOPING. Re-scoping `validateWallFirstTopology` but not `validateChainTopology` (or vice
     versa) leaves Room-move and chain authoring disagreeing — the exact pre-existing defect, moved.
     Guard: S4 and S5 are separate commits and acceptance requires both accounted for.
R-4  AUTHORING REGRESSION. Withdrawing adoption too widely breaks legitimate within-group extension.
     Guard: T13, plus the deliberate-splitters check in INV-6.
R-5  INGRESS REGRESSION. Import/duplicate parity can be over-tightened and reject documents it should
     admit, or left loose and re-open F-C1/F-C6. Guard: A-5's class-1 checks both directions.
R-6  SCOPE CREEP. The most likely failure of THIS slice is growing a UI, a warning producer or a schema
     field. Guard: §2 OUT and P23B.10 MR-8's named exception.
R-7  POLICY-AS-IMPLEMENTATION. Downstream documents could cite E as shipped before this slice lands.
     Guard: until it lands, every dependent fixture stays labelled as its own class (P23B.0 §2.1).
```
