## 1. Purpose and non-goals

**Purpose.** Turn the P23B.0 symptom measurements into a **source-backed model of the real editing
pipeline**: which function does which geometric work, how often per gesture and per release, over
what extent (whole document vs affected subset), whether it samples curves, what it reads and what
it produces, which diagnostic mark owns it, and what it costs. In one pass the harvest must also
resolve, or bound precisely, the **curved-topology correctness mismatch** the P23B.0 evidence
surfaced, because a performance problem cannot be reasoned about honestly on top of a validity gate
that admits geometry later gates reject.

**Non-goals (explicit).**

```text
no optimization, refactor or defect fix
no benchmark fixture, harness, baseline or budget change (P23B.0 owns those)
no new measurement rig, profiler, dashboard or tool
no numeric performance target and no enforced budget
no new architecture mechanism, guard, ratchet or contract
no schema, persisted-format or document change
no second geometry compiler or second compiled-geometry truth
no Rust/WASM migration decision (P23B.8 owns the decision; P23B.1 may only diagnose a hot path)
no reopening P23B.0, Phase 0, P23, or the architecture-cycle state
no final product policy for curved-crossing validity (present bounded options; owner decides in P23B.3)
no collapse of independent geometric passes without the equivalence proof §6.4 requires
```

**What "harvest" means here.** P23B.1 is a **discovery** slice in the umbrella's own child inventory.
Its output is durable *evidence and a map*, not a change. Anything that changes behaviour belongs to
P23B.4–P23B.9 after the owner ratification gate; anything that changes measurement infrastructure
belongs to P23B.0.

### 6.3 The primary question

**Is the same curved representation — or equivalent geometric work — recomputed independently
across topology validation, face extraction, compile and mesh preparation?**

The harvest must answer this with evidence, specifically:

- how many times `wallCenterlineSamples`/`sampleSegment` is invoked per release, and from which
  callers, for the *same* Wall and the *same* centerline;
- whether the sampled crossing gate, the compiler, face extraction, reconciliation and the mesh
  builder each re-derive the same polyline (and whether they can even share one, given their
  different input objects: baseline document vs candidate document vs compiled geometry);
- whether the pre and post topology gates sample the same Wall twice within one release;
- whether candidate geometry and baseline geometry are derived from the same inputs or from
  distinct objects that merely happen to be equal.

The harvest must state, per candidate sharing opportunity, the **input objects compared** and
whether equality of the *inputs* (not of the produced numbers) can be established from source.

### 6.4 What the harvest must NOT assume

```text
- that overlapping marks are the same work, or that they may be summed;
- that any two passes may be collapsed because they "look" equivalent;
- that a cheaper path is safe because it is cheaper.
```

For every sharing/collapsing opportunity the harvest identifies, it must state the **equivalence
proof obligation** that would have to be satisfied first — e.g. "the two passes must be shown to
consume the same normalized centerline under the same canonical orientation and the same sampling
options, and a test must prove byte-identical outputs for cache-hit vs recompute". Naming the
obligation is required; satisfying it is a later slice's job.

## 7. H3 — Cache, invalidation and derived-artifact ownership

**Goal.** A source-backed inventory of every derived artifact on the path, with its true lifetime
and invalidation key, so later reuse work maps dependency edges instead of guessing.

### 7.1 Required per-artifact record

For each artifact:

```text
source authority            the authoritative input(s) the artifact is derived from
derived artifact            what is produced
cache / lifetime            where it lives (state field / WeakMap / local), and how long it survives
                            (per call / per gesture / per geometry identity / per session)
invalidation key            the exact equality test that decides reuse; and what it does NOT cover
current reuse               does it already get reused today? on which path?
current rebuild             when is it rebuilt? how often per release?
potential reuse             where the same artifact is (or could be) rebuilt from equal inputs
proof obligation            what must be proven before any new reuse of it is safe
```

### 7.2 Coverage required

```text
compiled geometry             (CompiledLayoutGeometry + the accepted compile carried in PrecisionPlan)
curve samples                 (sampleSegment output; per-Wall sampled centerlines)
topology-derived data          (sampled crossing results; chord classification results)
face extraction intermediates  (extractBoundaryCandidateFaces output; canonical boundary cycle keys)
reconciliation intermediates   (correspondence components; reconcileRooms result)
snap indexes                   (derivedWallSnapIndex WeakMap; the merged Wall spans)
mesh geometry                  (derivedWallMeshes WeakMap; per-Room / per-Wall meshes; pick index)
Plan render model              (buildPlanRenderModel output; the PlanPreviewModel)
candidate vs baseline geometry (which object is which, and whether they can alias)
accepted compile reuse          (PreviewCompileReuse + reusesAcceptedCompile's canonical-JSON test)
```

### 7.3 What the evidence already fixes

The inventory must reflect, without re-litigating it:

- **baseline restore and accepted-compile reuse are effective** — the WeakMap mesh cache and the
  canonical-JSON acceptance-reuse test already avoid the rebuild they were designed to avoid;
- **candidate release still performs expensive fresh work** — this is where the release cost lives;
- **Junction resolution, warmed snap, Plan model and JSON stringify are not current leading
  suspects** — they are inventoried, but the inventory must not promote them to candidates.

### 7.4 What the inventory must NOT do

The harvest must **not** promote room-local, wall-local or incremental reuse merely because it
appears cheaper. It must first map every dependency edge: which artifacts depend on the whole
document, which on a single Wall, which on a single Room, and which on a compiled geometry object
whose identity is the real cache key. An artifact is only "room-local" if the source proves nothing
outside that Room can affect it. Where the dependency edge is `UNKNOWN`, the harvest records
`UNKNOWN` and names what would resolve it.

## 8. H4 — Curved-topology correctness mismatch

**A separate stabilization finding, not a performance item.** It is recorded here because a
performance model built on a gate that admits geometry later gates reject is not trustworthy.

### 8.1 What the P23B.0 evidence already establishes (settled)

```text
- E2 (the original 40-Wall fixture) contains genuine curved-Wall crossings: chords do not cross,
  but Bézier bows do;
- ordinary Wall authoring/edit operations rescan curved topology and REJECT because of that
  pre-existing crossing;
- Room duplication validates via endpoint/chord topology and can therefore ADMIT a curved copy whose
  actual Bézier geometry crosses another Wall;
- therefore a SUCCESSFUL operation can create geometry that a LATER Wall operation rejects — a
  correctness / stabilization defect.
```

### 8.2 The exact behaviour the harvest must establish

For each of the following ingress/edit surfaces, trace the actual validation chain:

```text
Room duplicate / repeat         planDuplicateIsolatedRoom + its batch gates
Wall-chain / rectangle authoring  planWallChain / planWallSegment + validateChainTopology
Bend and knot editing            planBendWallCurveKnot / planMoveWallCurveKnot /
                                 planInsertWallCurveKnot / planDeleteWallCurveKnot
import / codec validation        decodeLayoutJsonCompatible → validateWallFirstLayoutDocument
                                 → importLayoutPreviewJson
canonical compile                compileLayoutGeometrySource / compileWallFirstLayoutGeometry
topology gates                   validateWallFirstTopology + detectWallCurveTopologyCrossings
```

and answer, precisely and with source anchors:

```text
Q1  where exactly is a chord-disjoint but Bézier-crossing duplicate ACCEPTED — which gate, which
    function, and which check is absent there that the canonical path performs?
Q2  where does the resulting document FIRST become rejected — which operation, which gate, which
    rejection code?
Q3  do unrelated LATER Wall operations fail SOLELY because of the pre-existing crossing (i.e. is the
    failure caused by geometry the successful earlier operation introduced)?
Q4  does import intentionally permit such a document? Is admission at ingress deliberate (a
    compatibility/leniency decision) or is the topology gate simply not part of the codec path?
Q5  which canonical invariant currently OWNS curved-crossing validity — the codec, the topology
    gate, the chain authoring path, or several with divergent scope? Name the owner(s) and the
    divergence.
```

### 8.3 Required regression-test proposals

The harvest must *propose* (not implement) at least these, with the exact fixture shape and the
assertion:

```text
R-a  curved copies whose chords are disjoint but whose curves cross → the minimal reproduction of
     the mismatch: state the curve shape, the copy delta, the CURRENT observed verdict and the gate
     that produced it. The post-fix expected outcome is deliberately NOT preselected — rejection and
     explicit-invalid representation both stay open until the owner ruling (§8.4) — so R-a must pin
     the reproduction and the current behaviour without asserting which resolution it tests;
R-b  duplicate/repeat atomic rejection with NO history mutation — a rejected batch leaves document,
     history and identity unchanged (rejection and no-op both leave no history entry);
R-c  a later unrelated edit against a document that already carries a pre-existing curved crossing —
     states plainly whether the failure is caused by the pre-existing geometry, and how the
     operation reports it;
R-d  import behaviour for an unsupported-topology document — which of admit / reject / admit-as-
     invalid is the CURRENT behaviour, and where it is decided.
```

Test naming and lane placement follow `apps/editor/tests/README.md`; the harvest proposes the tests
and does not add them.

### 8.4 Bounded product-policy options (owner decision, NOT taken here)

The harvest presents the options and their consequences; it chooses none:

```text
OPTION A  reject at ingress — every surface (duplicate, authoring, import) applies the one
          sampled-curve validity rule, so an invalid document can never be created or admitted;
OPTION B  admit with an explicit invalid state — the document may exist and render, but is visibly
          marked invalid and Wall operations report the pre-existing cause rather than a fresh
          rejection;
OPTION C  provide repair — the system offers an explicit resolution (e.g. separate/nudge/noding
          decision) when an operation would produce a crossing, instead of silently rejecting;
OPTION D  (must be considered and may be rejected) leave behaviour as-is and document it.
```

The owner decision is deferred to P23B.3 (synthesis). P23B.1 must state, for each option, the
surfaces it would touch and the evidence the harvest has already gathered for it — no more.

## 11. Correctness-defect investigation contract

```text
EVIDENCE STANDARD
  every claim about rejection or admission cites the exact function + the exact check that decides
  it; "the code appears to" is not evidence. Where behaviour cannot be established from source
  alone, the harvest proposes the smallest read-only experiment that would establish it and records
  the question as open.

SCOPE
  investigation only. No gate, codec, planner, planner comment or test is modified. No fixture is
  committed. A gate whose COMMENT claims a bar it does not meet is recorded as a finding (for
  example, a batch gate asserting it holds "the same bar" as the canonical path) but the comment is
  NOT edited here.

ATOMICITY
  the harvest verifies (by source, and by any read-only experiment it runs) whether duplicate/repeat
  rejection is atomic with respect to history, document and identity, and reports it; it does not
  fix a violation it finds.

SEVERITY FRAMING
  the mismatch is presented as a stabilization/correctness finding with a reproduction, separate
  from the performance findings, and is never folded into a cost number.

DEFERRAL
  the final product policy is explicitly left to the owner in P23B.3. The harvest may recommend, but
  must present all bounded options with their consequences and mark the choice OPEN.
```

## 13. P23B.2 research handoff — targeted research brief

P23B.1 ends by handing P23B.2 a brief built **only from diagnosed questions** — questions the
harvest can now state precisely because it has named the function, the extent and the dependency
edge involved. Each topic must be attached to its H2/H3/H4 diagnosis; a topic with no diagnosis does
not go in the brief.

```text
T1  broad-phase acceleration for curved-segment topology
    Diagnosis: the sampled crossing gate compares Wall pairs; on the 40-Wall all-curved fixture,
    topology validation is a leading measured cost. Question: how do mature geometry systems
    broad-phase curved-segment intersection (spatial index / AABB tree / sweep) while preserving
    exact-equivalence semantics?

T2  reuse of canonical sampled-curve representations
    Diagnosis: wallCenterlineSamples → sampleSegment is reached independently from topology, compile,
    face extraction, reconciliation and mesh; summed curve sampling is a large share of release.
    Question: what is the established pattern for one canonical sampled representation shared across
    validation and rendering pipelines, and how is its equivalence (tolerance, sample count,
    orientation) guaranteed?

T3  incremental / dependency-aware validation and compilation
    Diagnosis: acceptance runs whole-document structural → topology → face → reconcile → topology →
    opening/portal → compile, twice for topology, per release. Question: how do mature systems scope
    revalidation and recompilation to the affected Wall/Room/face subgraph, and what invariants make
    a scoped revalidation equivalent to a full one?

T4  derived mesh caching and lifetimes
    Diagnosis: mesh derivation is already WeakMap-cached by geometry identity and demonstrably reused
    for baseline restore, yet candidate release still does fresh mesh work. Question: what cache
    keying/lifetime strategies keep candidate and baseline meshes distinct while still reusing
    unchanged sub-parts?

T5  candidate-vs-baseline geometry reuse
    Diagnosis: baseline restore and accepted-compile reuse are effective; the release candidate is
    the expensive fresh path. Question: how do systems reuse the unchanged part of a document
    between a baseline and its candidate without weakening the equivalence guarantee?

T6  Workers for isolated CPU-heavy work
    Diagnosis: the demonstrated hitch is a synchronous release/acceptance block on the main thread.
    Question: which of the diagnosed stages are naturally isolatable to a Worker under the one-
    compiler / one-truth invariant, and what is the realistic structural cost?

T7  Rust/WASM — ONLY IF a compute-bound hot path remains
    Diagnosis: not yet established; algorithmic and TypeScript opportunities are not yet understood.
    Question deferred until P23B.4–P23B.7 outcomes are known. P23B.1 makes NO migration decision;
    P23B.8 owns the decision and "not justified" is a valid close.
```

**Handoff rule.** P23B.2 researches the diagnosed questions; it does not re-diagnose. P23B.1 supplies
the diagnosis, the anchors and the open questions; where a diagnosis is `UNKNOWN`, the brief says so
and names what the harvest could not establish.
