## 1. The distinction this slice must respect

```text
CLOSABLE ≠ CLOSED.  P23B.10's acceptance makes the phase closable. Closure is invoked by the owner and
                    executes the `phase-closeout` skill (P-level status, baton, close preflight).
GREEN ≠ MERGE.      P23B.9's gate going green does not authorize a merge. The owner merges.
PHASE ≠ SLICES.     Approving one slice never approved a later one; each slice carries its own review.
HISTORY ≠ SQUASH.   Slice boundaries are reviewable artifacts; squashing them away destroys the review
                    trail this contract required. The active `main` ruleset requires linear history and
                    therefore blocks a merge commit. Do not change that ruleset automatically: at final
                    P1 closeout the owner must explicitly authorize a targeted temporary exception with
                    exact restoration, or rule on a different anchor-preserving merge method.
```

## 5. Risks

```text
R-1  FALSE COMPLETENESS. A gate that passed at a slice's HEAD is not a gate that passes at the branch
     HEAD. C2 exists because the optimizations interact.
R-2  HISTORY LOSS. Squashing or rebasing the phase away destroys the slice review trail. MR-1/MR-11 and
     the no-squash rule exist for this.
R-3  KNOWLEDGE DRIFT. Leaving a mechanism only in a slice plan means the next phase re-discovers it, or
     worse, contradicts it. C3 decides deliberately, in either direction.
R-4  ACCIDENTAL CYCLE CHANGE. Writing anything other than `CYCLE TARGET: PHASE_1 (unchanged)` would
     imply P23B changed the architecture cycle, which it must not.
R-5  HIDING A DEFECT. If the curved-crossing ruling is "leave as-is", the record must present it as an
     ACCEPTED, DOCUMENTED defect — not as an absence of one.
R-5b POLICY AS IMPLEMENTATION. The record states a selected policy as though it were the shipped
     behaviour, or a planned slice as though it had landed. Under Option E this is the most likely
     closeout error: E reads as a decision the phase made, while the validator is untouched. C5 names the
     three facts separately (policy · shipped state · delivery target) and R-7's discipline applies.
R-6  RE-OPENED SCOPE. Closeout is not the place to fix something. A discovered defect becomes a debt row
     or a new phase item, not an unplanned change inside the closeout commit.
R-7  UNDONE CORRECTION. Closeout RESTORES a claim the phase already retracted — most plausibly by
     re-asserting that the P23B.0 historical reports or the P23B.2 research report is missing (both are
     landed, and the retraction is P23B.3 §1.2 correction C-1). This is a live risk precisely because a
     stale instruction to say so once existed in C7; it was corrected there, and the corrected evidence
     state is binding on the PR description and on any closeout prose.
```
