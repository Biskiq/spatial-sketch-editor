T1 next batch: interaction sketches

Six static sketches for the design questions that decide the most. They use the existing PLATE vocabulary only: black ink for built Walls, cobalt dashes for a proposal, red dashes for a refusal, disc knobs for points, dark mono tags for live values, the Card's scoped list and the Stage state pill. They are sketches, not specifications. Part of the T1 next-batch proposal.

1. Split and join: where does a joint come from, and how does it go away?

Question: can Junction editing ship without a new tool, a new mark or an armed Card action?

Recommended. No Junction tool and no selectable Junction subject. Split from the scoped list; join by the existing removal gesture and the Eraser. The one grammar edit is a sentence in the Wall row extending bend-point removal to a two-Wall joint.

2. Duplicate a Room that shares a Wall

Question: what happens to the shared boundary and the door through it?

Recommended. One meaning for Duplicate regardless of neighbours. The isolated case ships first on the existing planner; the shared case adds a copy-enclosure planner, not topology surgery.

3. Connecting to a circle: three models

Question: which of these is “a simple connection”, and which is a new architectural model?

4. Radius editing on a connected rotunda (model B)

Question: when the radius changes, do the neighbours follow, or does the edit refuse?

5. Face this wall, and Unroll the room

Question: is elevation editing a new mode, or two readings the shell already names?

Recommended. No “Focus” mode and no new surface. Face lands near-term in the Card's Look row. Unroll the room is planned after the connected-curve decision, because under model B it is how a connected rotunda is seen whole.

6. Walls and overhead: meets, gap, intended

Question: can the first overhead slice be useful with no roof forms and no Wall change?

Recommended. V1 first: it is finished-looking over today's Walls, and it forces the D2 and D3 decisions while the geometry is trivial. A sloped Wall top under today's flat derived lid would look broken.
