Clear, visually pleasing, self-explanatory. Every control's shape says what it does: an arrow drags that way, a curved arrow turns, a knob on a stem lifts, a four-way cross moves, a chip toggles a mode. Drawing is solid ink (black or white); a proposal is dashed in the ghost colour, for every subject and in both views; a refusal is red dashed; selection is gold. 1 · Draw ink, snap cue, refusal

One black line, a black end mark, one length tag. The end mark's shape is the snap cue: ● free · ○ on a Wall or end · □ on the grid. Swing a segment across the Wall: placed segments stay; only the live one turns red dashed.

2 · Drag preview ghost of a Wall edit (2D)

The source stays put. The proposal is one dashed centreline per changed Wall — the same line you drew, dashed in the ghost colour. Rooms and objects use the same dashed language (section 8). Drag an end knob or the two-way arrow.

Ghost

Drag ● to move a junction (the neighbour follows) · drag ⟷ to slide the Wall · Esc while dragging cancels.

3 · Object gizmo move, turn, W·D·H (3D)

No ring: one bent two-way arrow beside the object turns it, and travels with it as it turns. Every gesture shows the object's dashed edges at the proposed pose in the ghost colour; the object itself stays put until release. The chip does two things: click shows the W·D·H arrows and the height stem, drag scales the whole object uniformly. The stem has two knobs: the top one moves the top, the bottom one lifts the object off its support (objects only). Values are deltas from where the gesture began.

8 · Transform ghosts one language for Walls, Rooms and objects (2D shown; 3D draws the same lines)

While a gesture is live, the subject stays where it is and its proposal is drawn dashed in the ghost colour; releasing commits it as one Undo step, Esc drops it. Walls and Rooms show their dashed centrelines. Objects show their dashed outline: the footprint in 2D, the box edges in 3D (section 3). A turn shows the same outline at the new angle. A refusal turns the ghost red dashed with its reason. Moving a Room moves its connected group: Rooms that share a Wall travel together, because they cannot be pulled apart without detaching architecture.

Objects in a moved Room

Drag ✥ on a Room to move its group · drag ✥ on an object (or its body) to move it · drag its bent arrow to turn it (15° steps, Alt for free) · drag the Wall's ⟷ to slide it · drop anywhere to commit · drag a Room onto the free Wall to see a refusal.

Objects in a moved Room. A Room and its Walls are Layout; furniture is Scene, placed in the world and owned by no Room. A keeps that split: the Room moves, objects stay (move them after). B moves objects standing in the Room with it as one Undo step; it needs one change that spans Layout and Scene, so it is a larger follow-up.
