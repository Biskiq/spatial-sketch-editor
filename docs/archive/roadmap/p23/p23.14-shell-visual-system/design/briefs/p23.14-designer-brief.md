# P23.14 — Editor Shell & Visual System Foundation: designer brief

**Status:** design exploration brief. **Not** a design specification, **not** an implementation plan, **not** a visual direction. **Date:** 2026-09-17.
**Audience:** independent external product/UI designers working on Museum Editor's editor shell.

This brief commissions concept exploration. It does not choose a direction, rank options, or
pick a winner. Choosing happens later, after concepts exist and are compared on equal terms.

**How to read it.** Three documents belong together, in this authority order:

1. **[`P23.14-shell-design-context.md`](../../context/shell-design-context.md) is authoritative** for product, architecture, ownership and scope. If anything here, in the research, or in your own instincts conflicts with it, the context wins and the conflict should be surfaced rather than silently resolved.
2. **[`P23.14-editor-shell-visual-system-research.md`](../../../../../../roadmap/p23-layout-depth/p23.14-shell-visual-system/research/editor-shell-visual-system.md)** is **evidence and inspiration** — precedent patterns, anti-patterns, visual-system dimensions and candidate territories. It is not a specification and must not be treated as a menu.
3. **This brief is the assignment**: the problem, the freedom, the constraints, what to produce, and how the work will be judged.

Everything in the context's §9 (contracts) and §11 (settled baseline) is out of your authority
to reopen. Everything else about the shell's presentation is your territory.

---

## 1. Purpose

Museum Editor is a spatial-authoring product that lets people build a museum's architecture in
Plan and 3D, stage models, materials and lights inside it, and author camera tours through it.
Its substance is finished well past its shell: the architectural Build phase (P23.0–P23.13)
delivered wall-first topology, direct editing, display identity and a settled Plan drafting
language — while the shell around that substance only ever settled far enough to be functional.

**P23.14 is the shell and visual-system slice.** The outcome it is accountable for:

> Establish a coherent, extensible editor-shell **visual system and interaction grammar** with a
> high enough ceiling that future capability normally enters through the *same language* instead
> of requiring a new shell redesign.

The desired end state, in one sentence:

> Museum Editor should feel like **one coherent, distinctive professional spatial-authoring
> product** whose visual and interaction language can grow with future capabilities instead of
> being redesigned every time the roadmap expands.

P23.14 owns the *visual operating system*. It does **not** implement future features: P24
staging capability, P26 spatial instruments and P23.15 junction geometry are out of scope. What
P23.14 owes them is a place to land (§10).

**This is not a reskin.** Proposals are expected to substantially reconsider composition,
hierarchy, density, surfaces, typography, iconography, state treatment and the
canvas/chrome relationship. A proposal that amounts to restyled component-library chrome has not
answered the brief.

---

## 2. Product character

The shell should support a product that feels:

- **professional** — precise, trustworthy, deterministic, with clear ownership of every value;
- **precise** — measurements, units, references and constraints treated as first-class;
- **educational and understandable** — a creator without CAD/DCC training can learn the product through the interface itself;
- **warm and friendly** — humane, approachable, inviting creative work;
- **creative** — a spatial and temporal authoring tool, not an enterprise form;
- **distinctive** — recognizable as Museum Editor, not as a generic UI kit or an imitation of another professional tool.

These are **qualities to explore**, not a style recipe, and they come from different mechanisms
rather than one compromise aesthetic. A useful target phrase from the research:
**“warm instrument, not friendly dashboard.”**

### Strong preference: minimalism is not the answer

Do not steer toward:

- generic sparse SaaS styling;
- “everything hidden until hover”;
- whitespace as the main design idea;
- monochrome component-library chrome;
- removing useful persistent information to make the UI look clean.

The product may be dense, expressive, layered and visibly instrumented. The goal is **structured
richness rather than clutter** — and the failure mode on the other side is equally real: dense
chrome with no ranking is not richness either.

## 3. Designer assignment

Each designer independently produces **2–3 genuinely distinct shell concepts**.

- Concepts must differ **structurally or philosophically**, not by palette or corner radius. Three
  variants of one idea is one concept.
- Each concept is a coherent thesis, not a mood board: personality, hierarchy, density, surfaces,
  domain/view/mode/tool treatment, Navigator, Inspector, Timeline, typography, color, iconography,
  canvas chrome, education, selection and state language, and extensibility, all decided
  consistently.
- Concepts will be compared against the **same states** (§12) and the **same tests** (§13), so
  comparability matters more than individual polish.
- Where you reject a research interpretation, combine ideas, find better precedents, or propose an
  entirely different visual thesis — do it, and **explain why**. Justified divergence is a
  contribution, not a deviation.

Required deliverables per concept: §11.

## 4. Fixed product and architecture contracts

These are non-negotiable. A concept that violates one cannot be accepted however good it looks.
Full text and rationale: design context §9.

1. **Scene / Camera is a domain switch** over **one** document world. It never changes the document, history or world, and never snaps the view.
2. **Plan / 3D are the current top-level spatial views.** They are one view axis, not two products, and they consume one compile.
3. **P26 may later add contextual orthographic instruments** (Section, Wall Elevation, Ceiling Focus). They must extend the existing grammar — not create a second navigation or camera system, and not become peer document worlds. **Do not hard-code exactly two permanent view slots.**
4. **One navigation system. One camera-motion system.** No parallel authority, ever.
5. **Camera Timeline is Camera-owned**, in both Plan and 3D, with persistent collapsed/expanded state and no auto-expansion on domain switch.
6. **`LayoutDocument` and `SceneDocument` are separate authorities.** Layout edits never move world-local Scene or Camera content implicitly. This is the single most load-bearing architectural fact for shell design.
7. **Scene placement is world-local.** Do not design affordances that imply Room-relative object ownership.
8. **One deterministic selection authority**, consumed by viewport, Navigator and Inspector. Never parallel selection truth — and never “exactly one selected entity” as the durable contract: P24 may extend Scene selection to an **ordered set with one primary/active entity** that every surface resolves identically. Selection identity belongs to the entity, not to a view; a view switch may change handles, never identity.
9. **The Inspector is selection-scoped.** It is the property authority for the current selection context, never a document inventory, and never becomes one by accretion.
10. **Derived presentation never becomes authored truth.** Footprints, dimensions, fills, section streams and previews are read-only and never feed architecture; equally, nothing invented as a purely visual layer may become required document state.
11. **Invalid input is refused with a reason** — never auto-repaired, never silently dropped. A silent no-op is a defect, not a style choice.
12. **One mechanism per question.** No second placement path, snap engine or geometry authority. A design that needs “a small parallel system” has found an architecture problem.
13. **The viewport remains the primary work surface.** The shell may be quiet or present; the canvas is not a preview pane.
14. **Visitor surfaces are not shell states.** Editor-only hierarchy, selection, history and gizmo code never enter visitor chunks.

Deterministic history and Undo/Redo span both domains; there is one chronological tagged history
stack. Do not design around implementation details beyond these boundaries.

## 5. Settled P23.13 baseline

**P23.13's architectural Plan language is not being redesigned.** It shipped and is settled. You
inherit it as an input, not as a discussion:

- architectural wall/opening/room drafting language — wall mass, joins, caps, boundary vs non-boundary distinction;
- neutral Door/Window semantics: opening symbols represent **only** authored data (swing, handing, leaf type are not inferred and must never be invented visually);
- handles and direct-edit grammar, including the keyboard control-group contract;
- semantic zoom / density shedding rules for dense plans;
- display identity — name and canonical reference system;
- snap / invalid / focus / selection-precedence semantics;
- authored-vs-derived truthfulness, including passive Scene footprints in Plan;
- the anchored numeric field, live dimension, snap marker and guide language.

You may redesign **how the shell relates to this canvas** — framing, contrast, chrome, density,
typography, surfaces. You may not casually reopen the drafting language itself, and you should
not treat its current Plan colors as disposable: selection, snap and invalid hues already carry
meaning.

Two forward-looking caveats:

- **P23.15 still owns junction-correct architectural 3D geometry.** Current Wall/Junction mesh
  appearance in 3D is **not** a final visual baseline. A concept may not depend on today's mesh
  appearance or compensate for current 3D seams, gaps or joins through shell presentation.
- **A concurrent slice already merged** (Junction dissolve / wall join, PR #57). Its shipped entry
  point is Delete/Backspace on a selected degree-2 Junction. Its *optional* entry points —
  an Inspector action, a Navigator row action, a Plan context-menu item — were deferred into
  P23.14's shell finish. Your Inspector and menu/popover grammar should therefore be able to host a
  **reason-coded destructive action** alongside property sections, and to show a refusal reason
  rather than a silent no-op.

## 6. Current shell and the screenshots

The current shell has three rows of geography — a Project Row, a Workspace Ribbon, and a status
bar — around a left rail (Navigator), a viewport and a right Inspector, with a Camera Timeline
docked under Camera 3D. Its anatomy, surface by surface, is described in design context §3.

Five screenshots in [`P23.14-shell-context/`](../../context/screenshots/) document what exists. They
are **orientation evidence, not a target**:

- screenshot 1 — Scene · Plan, Layout mode, selection in the rail;
- screenshot 2 — Scene · Plan, active wall operation (gesture, dimension, handles);
- screenshot 3 — Camera · 3D, **empty/no-flow** Timeline expanded (an empty dock, no lane surface);
- screenshot 4 — Camera Plan/3D, empty Timeline with the selected camera's finite frustum;
- screenshot 5 — Scene · 3D with an architectural Wall selected.

Two honest limits, stated so nobody designs around a gap:

- **The populated Timeline — ruler, the five lanes, numbered node diamonds, shot blocks, FOV keys,
  and the collapsed 48 px mini-player — is not captured.** It exists in shipped markup and styles,
  but a canonical-project placement defect currently blocks reaching it (recorded as TD-1). Do not
  read the empty docks as the intended Timeline design.
- **Zero captures show a dense working state.** That is exactly why §12 F is mandatory.

Current screenshots show what exists. They are not what the redesign should resemble.

## 7. Known visual and system problems

Condensed from design context §8, which carries evidence, status and P24/P26 exposure per row.
Cite the row number when you propose a fix.

| # | Problem (context §8 row) | What it needs from a concept |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Control-weight flatness.** Domain, view, local mode and tools sit adjacent at near-identical weight; four semantic ranks read as one run of chips (rows 1, 6) | A rank grammar that survives P24 tool growth and a third P26 view concept |
| 2 | **Inspector leads with documentation.** In Scene states the Inspector opens with a read-only summary and multi-line prose before any property; Camera does not — the inconsistency is itself the defect (row 2) | Property-first composition, and one cross-domain grammar |
| 3 | **One fact, several homes.** Snap/Grid appear in the ribbon, the status bar and inside the Plan canvas; navigation hints and scale/grid split across surfaces (row 3) | One authoritative location per fact, at most one passive echo |
| 4 | **“Preview” means four different things.** Row 1 visitor takeover, ribbon tour overlay, Camera rail preview sequence, publish-surface preview; plus a paint-only Room rotation handle that commits nothing (rows 4, 12) | Vocabulary discipline, and a trust test applied to every visible control |
| 5 | **Rail residue.** Truncated identity text, selection state mixed with navigation actions, non-selectable `Ends …` relation rows reading as entities (row 5) | Row species: entity, group heading, relation metadata, empty state |
| 6 | **Two canvas-chrome vocabularies.** Plan puts utilities and scale in-canvas bottom-right; 3D puts a navigation box top-right (row 6) | One canvas-utility grammar that P26 instruments can extend |
| 7 | **Timeline width fragmentation.** The collapsed mini-player does not align with the viewport, rails or status bar (row 7) | Timeline as part of the workspace grid, not an attached widget |
| 8 | **Accessibility gaps.** Status-bar hint contrast, canonical axis tokens in number fields, context-menu keyboard navigation, tablist behaviour, popover coordination, Document-menu focus lifecycle, an unmet coarse-pointer target (rows 8, 9, 11) | State and focus systems strong enough to fix these structurally, not one at a time |
| 9 | **Generic identity.** No signature visual trait; the tonal system establishes none (row 10) | A defensible identity thesis — see §13 Identity test |
| 10 | **Plan ↔ 3D continuity unproven**, and the lane surface vanishes without a flow while the dock keeps its height (rows 13, 14) | Evidence that one product spans both views, and an authored empty state |

**Prior attempts already in the tree — do not repeat blindly:** Row 1/Row 2 relocation of
contextual toolbars; Row 2 panel-visibility grouping; focus mode `0 1fr 0`; P23.12 compact
references; P23.13 Plan language, semantic zoom and label-collision rules; the P23.6e
relationship-aware Navigator.

## 8. Research takeaways to expose, not to prescribe

The research exists so you are not starting from nothing. Its most transferable conclusions:

1. **Stable workbench landmarks + contextual contents.** Keep *where* kinds of work happen fixed; let the contents change.
2. **Semantic ranking matters more than reducing chrome.** Museum Editor's problem is not too much UI; it is too little ranking between kinds of UI.
3. **Canvas-first does not mean chrome-free.** Canvas dominance comes from rank and quiet, not from deletion.
4. **Progressive density beats progressive disappearance.** Add sections, grouping, filtering and disclosure before inventing hover-only UI.
5. **One fact, one authoritative home**, with at most one deliberately passive echo.
6. **Selection coherence across viewport, Navigator and Inspector can become a product signature** — and it is a fixed contract, so making it *visible* is free value.
7. **The Inspector should be property-first**, not documentation-first.
8. **Hierarchy must distinguish entities from relations and metadata** — and from empty-state teaching.
9. **Education belongs at uncertainty boundaries**: the moment of a gesture, a disabled action, a refusal, an empty state — not in permanent prose above the working controls.
10. **The Timeline should feel structurally integrated into the Camera workspace**, sharing the shell's grid, headers and state language.
11. **P26 creates a first-class “nested context with a return path” problem**: contextual instruments must not pretend to be new documents or new top-level tabs.
12. **Product identity should survive theme changes.** With seven themes in play, identity that lives in palette alone is not identity.

A statement that outranks the rest, reached independently by both research passes:

> The first obligation is a **visual grammar for rank** —
> `project → domain → view → contextual instrument → local mode → tool → selection → gesture/status`.
> Whether the shell is light or dark matters far less than whether those lifetimes stop looking
> like eight equal buttons.

You may **reject** any of these interpretations, **combine** compatible ones, **identify better
precedents**, or propose a different thesis altogether — provided you explain the reasoning and
show how your concept satisfies the contracts in §4.

### 8.1 Candidate territories are provocations, not a menu

The research names five territories — **Instrumented Atelier**, **Technical Workbench**,
**Chromatic Studio**, **Editorial Control Room**, **Guided Spatial Desk** — as deliberately
different personality/density/identity positions. Treat them as **vocabulary and provocation
only**. You are not required to choose one. You may reinterpret one, hybridize compatible
principles, reject all five, or introduce a stronger territory. They are not ranked, and the
research explicitly declines to pick one.

### 8.2 Open questions are yours to answer

The design context's §17 and the research's §9 / §9.1 collect questions neither document answers
— from density targets at 240–300 px rail width, to the difference between mode, armed tool,
selected entity, keyboard focus and pressed control, to how the no-flow Timeline body should read
without inventing fake lanes. Your concepts should **take positions** on them rather than avoid
them. Where a question is really a product decision (for example removing a Timeline lane), the
concept should raise it, not settle it silently.

## 9. Freedom to challenge

You are explicitly free to reconsider, replace or restructure:

- overall shell composition and the current two-row arrangement;
- toolbar grouping, labels and headers;
- panel styling, borders, corner geometry, elevation, surface hierarchy;
- Navigator composition and hierarchy treatment;
- Inspector composition, section order and density;
- Timeline framing, integration and collapsed/expanded treatment;
- status-bar presentation and canvas chrome placement;
- typography, spacing, density, alignment rhythm;
- iconography — noting the current set is genuinely mixed (a Lucide-derived family, owner-ratified custom Plan icons, and repo Plan icons from P23.13);
- shape language, dividers, color strategy and accent policy;
- domain / view / mode visual treatment, including whether they are separated spatially, typographically or structurally at all;
- menus, popovers, overlays, empty states, refusal and warning treatments;
- contextual education mechanisms;
- interaction-state presentation, including how states overlap.

Panel geometry and float are free where ownership survives. Docked-versus-floating is a
presentation decision; who *owns* a value is not.

**The current Svelte/component structure must not constrain the design ceiling.** Existing CSS,
tokens, component boundaries and screenshots are not visual constraints merely because they exist.
Implementation concerns are not the designer's problem at this stage — but flagging an
implementation-sensitive assumption in your risks section is welcome and useful.

## 10. Future boundaries: P24, P26, P23.15

P24, P26 and P23.15 are used here to **test extensibility**. Do not design their features.

### P24 — Scene staging depth

Your shell must plausibly absorb, later: ordered multi-selection with one primary entity; mixed
property values; substantially more property sections; materials; lights and environment;
placement and support refusal reasons; a larger Scene hierarchy; and destructive actions such as
an environment reset.

You are **not** asked to design those workflows. You are asked to demonstrate that your visual
system could take them without a rewrite: a selection header that can carry one, many and mixed;
a property row grammar that can express mixed, inherited and derived values distinctly; a
hierarchy that can grow without inventing a second tree and without contradicting world-local
Scene ownership; a reason surface that works both near a gesture and inside a property panel;
destructive actions that read as consequential rather than as toggles.

### P26 — Architectural Spatial Depth

Your shell must plausibly absorb, later: Section, Wall Elevation and Ceiling Focus-style
instruments; derived cut/projected/hidden streams; crop/depth/reveal concepts; contextual
precision instruments; and entry/exit with a strong return path.

You are **not** asked to decide P26's final view taxonomy. Crucially:

> **The shell must not hard-code exactly two permanent view slots** in a way that makes P26 require
> another redesign.

P23.14 should make the future answer **hostable**, not decide it. A concept that solves today's
Plan/3D pair with a two-option switcher and offers no plausible home for a third contextual
instrument has not met the brief.

### P23.15 — junction-correct wall-first 3D

P23.15 owns derived 3D architectural geometry. Current Wall/Junction mesh appearance is not final,
and the shell may not hide current 3D seams through presentation tricks. Concepts must still read
correctly after that geometry changes.

## 11. Required concept deliverables

For **each** concept, deliver the following. Depth over volume; annotated diagrams, mockups and
composition sketches are all acceptable.

### 1. Thesis

A short statement: what the product feels like; what makes the design recognizable; its main
hierarchy principle; and why it suits Museum Editor — including why it fits a creator without
CAD/DCC training.

### 2. Shell composition

Global/project frame; Scene / Camera treatment; Plan / 3D treatment; Layout / Arrange treatment;
tool region; Navigator; Inspector; Timeline; status and canvas chrome. State explicitly **which
elements are persistent and which are contextual**, and where anything new enters.

### 3. Visual system

Typography, density, surfaces, shape language, borders and elevation, spacing, icon treatment,
color roles, state grammar and motion principles. Exact production tokens are not required unless
they help communicate intent — but the system must be described precisely enough to be judged for
consistency and for ceiling.

### 4. Educational model

How the concept helps a non-CAD/DCC creator understand active context, tool state, property
ownership, disabled actions, rejected operations and unfamiliar concepts — **without filling the
Inspector with permanent tutorial prose**.

### 5. Extension model

How the concept absorbs P24 and P26 pressure without inventing a second visual system. Demonstrate
the slots; do not design the features.

### 6. Risks

Where the concept could become cluttered; where it could become too sterile; accessibility risks
(including color independence, focus visibility and coarse pointer); density risks; identity risks;
and any implementation-sensitive assumptions that should be checked later.

### Also acceptable and encouraged

Side-by-side comparison sketches, annotated current-state critiques, a state matrix, a type/density
scale, or a one-page “why this is not a reskin” argument. Effort should go to **whole-shell
composition, hierarchy, real dense states and system coherence** before polishing individual
controls. Production-polish fidelity is not required from every concept — the comparison is between
strong systems and visual theses, not illustration quality.

## 12. Canonical review states A–F

Every concept must be demonstrated against the **same six states**, which mirrors design context §15
(the source of truth, including which content is P24/P26-sensitive). Use them so proposals are
comparable.

| # | State | Must be shown |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Scene · Plan — normal architectural editing** | domain, view, Layout/Arrange rank, tool region, Navigator with grouped architecture, Plan canvas (ruler/grid/wall mass/room label), Inspector for the selection, status bar |
| **B** | **Scene · Plan — active architectural operation** | armed tool, gesture feedback (ghost, live dimension, snap marker, guide), handles, refusal state for an invalid gesture, and how much shell may dim or yield during work |
| **C** | **Scene · 3D — object selected** | selection coherence across viewport, rail and Inspector; transform group and `Local`/`World`; 3D canvas utilities. Treat the architectural mass around the object as P23.15-sensitive |
| **D** | **Camera · Plan — path authoring** | Camera-domain identity, camera authoring controls, Camera sidebar sections, read-only architectural backdrop, camera nodes/connections — and how the product still reads as one product with Scene |
| **E** | **Camera · 3D + Timeline** | Timeline integrated into the shell grid; **expanded with a flow** (ruler + five lanes + node diamonds + shot blocks + FOV keys); **no-flow/empty** treatment; **collapsed (48 px)** treatment; and a selected camera node's frustum in the viewport. Camera Timeline semantics are not yours to change |
| **F** | **Dense/high-pressure state — mandatory** | enough rails, hierarchy, properties, warnings and controls that the concept cannot survive on an empty hero screenshot: a many-room/many-wall plan, expanded rail groups, a populated Inspector, labels at minimum zoom. This is the real density ceiling |

**Comparison discipline.** Judge each concept on A–F **together**. A concept that wins on A
(clean and quiet) but fails C and F has not won. F is not optional; it is the state that separates a
visual thesis from a styling exercise.

## 13. Concept-review pressure tests

Each concept must answer these explicitly. They mirror design context §16.

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Semantic hierarchy** | Can a user distinguish `project → domain → view → local mode → tool → selection → transient state` **without reading every label**? |
| **Canvas** | Does the viewport still read as the primary work surface — quieter shell, louder canvas in working states? |
| **Selection** | Does one identity stay coherent across canvas, Navigator and Inspector, with no second selection truth? |
| **Density** | Does the system stay legible when panels hold substantial real information — and does it degrade by shedding rather than overflowing or hiding essentials? |
| **P24** | Could selection sets, mixed properties and additional staging sections fit later without breaking the rank grammar or the selection authority? |
| **P26** | Could contextual spatial instruments enter without a second navigation system, a second visual language, or a shell replacement? |
| **Timeline** | Does the Camera Timeline feel native — aligned, structurally owned, correct in all three states? |
| **Education** | Can the interface explain unfamiliar or refused state without permanent tutorial clutter? |
| **Trust** | Does every visible control communicate what it does — or why it currently cannot act? No mute controls, no silent no-ops, no prose standing in for a property. |
| **Ownership** | Can a creator always tell which surface owns the value they are changing, with each fact in exactly one authoritative home? |
| **Collapse** | Does the shell still work with both side panels collapsed — selection legible, way back obvious? |
| **Identity** | Does the concept feel recognizably like Museum Editor rather than a generic UI kit or an imitation of another professional tool — and would that identity survive a theme change? |
| **P23.15** | Does any part of the concept depend on today's Wall/Junction 3D appearance being final, or hide current seams through shell styling? |
| **Label discipline** | Does the concept introduce **zero** new naming or labelling schemes beyond the existing display-identity contract? |

## 14. Explicit non-goals

This brief must not:

- choose the winning visual direction or rank the candidate territories;
- prescribe minimalism, or any of its opposites as a rule;
- require copying Blender, Figma, Unreal, Ableton or any other precedent;
- ask designers to preserve the current shell arrangement;
- ask designers to implement P24 or P26 features, or to solve P23.15 geometry;
- reopen the P23.13 Plan drafting language;
- specify CSS, tokens or component architecture;
- optimize for current component boundaries;
- treat the five screenshots as authoritative, or treat the current empty Timeline dock as intended design;
- turn “professional” into CAD cosplay, “friendly” into oversized rounded consumer cards, “creative” into decoration, or “educational” into permanent explanatory text;
- collapse every design choice into one recommended concept before exploration has happened;
- write the final P23.14 design specification — that comes later, from whichever concept(s) survive comparison.

A concept may be bold, dense, expressive or unusual. What it may not be is unranked, incoherent
across the two domains, dependent on hidden state, or unable to grow.
