# What Biskiq's agents were told, and what the evidence proved

Research for the PR6 proposal, 26 November 2026. The baseline is the merge of PR5 into main, `2ca766335`. This report records observations at that revision. Its recommendations are for review.

## Three explanations that can be tested

1. **Agents could find two incompatible answers to the same product question.** On U5, even the first screen of the shell reference contradicted the approved design. A rational implementer following that document could deliver the wrong Library.
2. **The accepted evidence could pass for either answer.** U5 verified real reads, writes, Undo and some browser behavior. The review boundary explicitly allowed the core visual decision to slip to a later PR. Removing all the conflicting text would still have left this escape.
3. **The cost of adding a rule is paid by someone other than its author.** A change can pass every link check while retiring the service a skill tells its next reader to use. Conversely, a code PR can exercise the link scanner while breaking its documentation-only entry point. Maintenance by occasional cleanup leaves both failure modes intact.

There are important counterexamples. An earlier repository audit found successful narrow routing. The later T1 and T2 programs made good use of extensive technical material. These observations argue for changing **which evidence reaches which decision**, keeping ambitious multi-PR blueprints and domain invariants while removing procedural paperwork directly rather than adding another evaluation framework. ## Why deep planning worked

- **T1 distinguished a wiring gap from an invention.** Several gestures only needed to reach an existing planner. Connecting a rotunda required comparing representations. S1 separated real topology behavior from simulated history and measured cubic subdivision error below `1e-11 m` at the sampled stations. At a proposed radius of 6.125 m a neighbour's opening no longer fit. Whole-edit refusal and unchanged history made a better design constraint than a preferred gesture. [Creator journeys](../docs/roadmap/t1-spatial-world/next-batch/creator-journeys.md) · [S1 methods and limits](../docs/roadmap/t1-spatial-world/next-batch/spikes/s1-connected-curves.md)
- **A failed experiment changed the plan.** In S2, removing two adjacent walls collapsed a corner under the zero-new-field proposal. The compiler and certified index gave distinct failure evidence. Explicit virtual edges became a candidate. The historical report names the production lifecycle suite that succeeded the throwaway harness. For roofs, the Vellum study separately shows why agreement between a mesh and its own evaluator missed rotation/translation failures. Fixtures, tolerances and independent comparisons carry more future value than the mandate that commissioned them. [Space conversion](../docs/roadmap/t1-spatial-world/next-batch/spikes/s2-space-conversion.md) · [Geometry library harvest](./geometry-library-harvest.md)
- **T2 separated an artistic promise from a rendering assumption.** The S0 study measured affordable sky updates alongside unacceptable indoor light leakage. Snapshot grey read 55 where the Stage read 128 until the color path was fixed. Those findings changed what the team could promise and which measurement it could trust. S8 then let designers explore *picking walnut* over the production material capabilities. E7's measurement method was reused in the thumbnail lab while its engineering presentation could be replaced. [S0 results](../docs/roadmap/t2-look-atmosphere/s0-rendering-gate/report.md) · [Appearance study, real and simulated behavior](../prototypes/appearance-ux/README.md)

## What transfers from external work

| Evidence and scope | Implication for Biskiq |
| --- | --- |
| **Controlled repository-task studies disagree on the headline because they measure different outcomes.** Gloaguen et al. tested 300 SWE-bench Lite and 138 CTXbench tasks. Generated instructions added 20% and 23% cost respectively, with no significant resolution gain. Developer files did better than generated ones on CTXbench, with no significant improvement over the no-file condition. Lulla et al., on 124 PR tasks in ten repositories, found median runtime 28.64% lower and output tokens 16.58% lower with instructions. Their efficiency study did not fully grade patch correctness. [Gloaguen et al., v3, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v3) · [Lulla et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404) | Keep surprising, consequential local instructions, such as the DB-backed API gate. Compare quality and cost together. A shorter input alone can shift the expense into exploratory reads. Neither benchmark tested fidelity to a Biskiq design. |
| **Staleness can cost time even when the task succeeds.** A November preprint varied three real instructions across 22 tasks and 732 trajectories. A stale test command cost one model about two extra tool calls per run. Two other cases had no detectable cost difference. [Stale-instruction experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09802) | Look for obsolete actions in successful runs. Removing a stale maturity instruction is a plausible efficiency gain here. Its local effect remains unmeasured. |
| **Skills can help substantially under favorable retrieval conditions.** SkillsBench v4 reports 33.9% to 50.5% mean pass rate with curated skills across 87 tasks and 18 model–harness configurations. A separate study with retrieval from 34,000 real skills found gains approached the no-skill baseline in its hardest settings. [SkillsBench v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670v4) · [Skill retrieval in the wild](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04323) | Keep specialized, demonstrated procedures available on demand. A capability gain with the right skill selected says little about how often a live client selects an irrelevant one. Test both natural triggers and a nearby task that should skip it. |
| **Schmid's recommendations evolved.** His AGENTS guide favors a concise orientation and borrows figures from an earlier paper revision. His skills articles distinguish preferences from capability gaps and compare task outcomes with and without a skill. His November post and companion article report that Gemini 4 Neon tolerated fewer rules and made good use of visual inputs. This is one practitioner's model-specific experience, including his compaction setting. [AGENTS guide](https://www.philschmid.de/writing-good-agents) · [Skills design](https://www.philschmid.de/agent-skills-tips) · [Skills testing](https://www.philschmid.de/testing-skills) · [Original post](https://x.com/_philschmid/status/2125579472594223340) · [Neon account](https://www.philschmid.de/gemini-4-neon-context-engineering) | Borrow the negative control and the visual input. Leave model settings, token thresholds and a prescribed number of eval prompts out of repository policy. The open skill specification describes metadata discovery and optional supporting files, without promising invocation accuracy. [Agent Skills specification](https://agentskills.io/specification) |
| **Two harness teams favor legibility with different costs.** Anthropic describes dynamic retrieval and curated initial context, with latency and discovery tradeoffs. OpenAI describes one greenfield product using an in-repo map, architectural checks, browser feedback and recurrent gardening. Its throughput estimate is an experience report. [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) · [OpenAI engineering](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/) | Adopt reachable evidence and executable boundaries. Biskiq already tried an archive-oriented gardener and a maturity pipeline. A second recurring maintenance service would need its own demonstrated benefit. |
| **Mature repositories allocate authority differently.** Chromium advises changing documentation with its code and deleting dead material. Rust reserves RFCs for substantial change and distinguishes an accepted design from implementation or priority. [Chromium documentation practice](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/docs/documentation_best_practices.md) · [Rust RFC lifecycle](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs#the-rfc-life-cycle) | Update the few consumers of a changed contract in the same PR. Keep a durable rationale for a hard-to-recover architectural choice. Rust's community consensus process and permanent RFC collection solve a different coordination problem. |
| **Visual fidelity has its own evidence.** Design2Code's 484 real webpage screenshots exposed missing elements and layout errors through rendered and human evaluation. These are static screens. GOV.UK uses realistic interactive prototypes to explore services while reserving production responsibilities for production code. Playwright documents that screenshot pixels can vary with environment. [Design2Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03163) · [GOV.UK prototyping](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/making-prototypes) · [Playwright comparisons](https://playwright.dev/docs/test-snapshots) | Compare named states and critical actions on the real Stage. Inspect hierarchy, target, responsive changes and outcome. Use a stable crop or image diff for a specific regression when appropriate. A fixed pixel cutoff over a variable 3D scene would answer the wrong question. |

The conclusion from these sources is straightforward: pair a small, authority-aligned set of invariants with direct access to approved visual specimens and active multi-PR blueprints, remove paperwork-generating procedures directly, and validate through ordinary development and review.
