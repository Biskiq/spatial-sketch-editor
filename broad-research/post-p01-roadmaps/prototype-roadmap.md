# Creative prototype program

The [coordination record](./README.md) owns capability dispositions, research triggers and authorization
routes. This document defines the creative questions and the evidence needed to judge them.
The [engineering roadmap](./engineering-authorization-roadmap.md) keeps justified foundations moving.

**Later owner verdict, 2026-10-21:** P1's Guide-free Presentation finding, P2 Smart Wall's intelligent
manipulation/revision and P3 Vellum's expressive sketching are accepted; P4 is deferred. This file
preserves the commissioning questions, not a demand to repeat the prototypes or evidence that every
listed feature was accepted. The [convergence proposal](../../docs/roadmap/product-convergence-2026-10-21.md)
and the coordination record now route the next work. In particular, the original early-P4 sequencing
below is inactive while P4 remains deferred.

The proposition to test is broader than a museum editor: a creator should be able to shape a spatial
world, explain something within it, invite meaningful interaction, and revise the result without
rebuilding it. Imported objects, imported architecture and native construction are equally legitimate
starting points.

## 1. Diverge first, synthesize second

**Divergent exploration.** Begin with a minimal creative problem, demanding source material and the
capability the creator must achieve. Offer competing interaction approaches, not a prescribed shell.
Stage-first manipulation, contextual gestures, storyboards, spacious invoked instruments and
intent-led proposals are all eligible. Head/Index/Stage/Card is not the template for every experiment.
Provide product goals, truthful capability limits and architectural invariants; do not supply a
production schema as the creative solution. Choose the number of approaches by decision value.

**Owner review and selective synthesis.** Exercise alternatives on the same content and revisions,
including a capable ordinary workflow. The owner judges intuition, creative control and fit with the
vision. Combine the strongest ideas selectively. Conservatory's Presentation/View/Guide workflow is
the accepted baseline and should survive unless an alternative meaningfully improves it; a different
surface is not itself a defect.

**Converged Biskiq design.** Translate accepted discoveries into existing semantic ownership. Determine
whether PLATE accommodates them, an invoked instrument suffices, or an explicit PLATE amendment is
needed. A concise synthesis may be enough; do not require another full prototype when the answer is
clear. Shell freedom during exploration does not ratify a new production shell.

**Technical and contract reconciliation.** Prove only the mechanisms required by the accepted
direction and its first consumer. Reuse existing evidence where sufficient. A simulated geometry
operation or control binding remains disposable evidence, never an implicit schema, evaluator or
production command.

No fixed schedules, staffing, participant quotas, UX scores or compulsory iteration counts apply.
Reviewers may recommend; only the owner records the UX verdict.

No prototype-framework PR precedes the first useful experience. Reuse prepared assets, fixtures,
tokens or runners where they help; extract shared infrastructure only after an actual prototype needs
it. Prototypes stay independently runnable and outside production workspaces. P1–P3 may use prepared
geometry and simulated behavior with explicit disclosure. P4's claimed edits require real semantic
operations.

### P1 — Interactive Presentation Studio

**Creator journey.** Start with a mechanical watch or comparably rich object in an otherwise empty
World. Inspect what it actually provides: addressable parts, useful pivots, supplied clips or only a
static mesh. Select a subject or relationship and try **Present this**: review a Camera-framing and
Presentation proposal, then change it deliberately. Compose explanatory text, close-up Views and
visitor controls; position and style controls, inspect targets, and bind events to supported actions.
Preview the Presentation and interact directly. Revise one control and one framing decision, then
reuse the explanation within a larger exhibition, with a Guide only if useful.

A second subject or an empty architectural sightline tests whether the workflow generalizes. A watch
editor or a good-looking object viewer with a Play button does not answer the question. A Presentation
is visitor-facing composition; the underlying capability or portable performance it invokes has its
own owner and lifetime.

**Genuinely different approaches to consider.** Contextual control creation against the actual 3D
subject; an invoked spacious control/binding instrument; visual moments or storyboard organization;
and intent-led generation followed by exact editing. Theme-generated controls are a serious simpler
comparator. Borrow the spatial-interface study's diegetic controls or component canvas without
adopting its third lens or website-building ontology. Compare screen controls, projected callouts and
supported 3D targets where each serves the content; they have different readability and hit-testing
costs. These are design prompts, not mandatory separate builds.

**Initial lifecycle to demonstrate.** Enter the Presentation's defined baseline; invoke an available
action in a distinct activity context; cancel that context on interruption, skip or exit. Its affected
runtime contribution resets according to the defined baseline/channel policy. Removing one run must
not overwrite authored World state, unrelated active contributions or a newer invocation. Re-entering
creates a fresh run; stale completion callbacks cannot reclaim output. This is the simple default for
bounded demonstrations, not a ban on independent performances or richer timing later.

**Revision and failure cases.**

- An “explode” action requested for an asset with separate meshes but no such capability is honestly
  unavailable. Repeated names do not establish component identity.
- Reframing makes a label obscure the mechanism. Move/style the control, then operate it by keyboard
  and touch without relying on hover; provide a readable semantic alternative to a 3D-only target.
- Interrupt halfway, repeat an action, leave and return; exercise a competing or unrelated contribution.
  Disclose whether reset/arbitration is real or simulated.
- Remove a bound subject/View or replace the asset with an incompatible one. Preserve the authored
  explanation and show repair; do not pick a similarly named replacement.
- Reuse with explicit “this use” versus shared reach, including repeated occurrences if a Guide is
  used. A local change must not silently alter every use.

**Owner decision.** Which surface makes control composition, targeting and reuse understandable while
preserving ordinary precision? Does the same method work without a Room or Guide and later within an
exhibition? Can the creator make a deliberately different result from the same asset? Would simple
content or a conventional page serve the task better?

**Next boundary.** Selected UI/content grammar, imported identity or activity lifecycle opens only the
needed RR-5/6/3 questions. Existing Camera/Preview foundations do not wait. The program does not make
T3.P02 absorb rich controls, invent missing clips, demand a timeline/state inspector, or authorize
scroll/embed delivery. Rich conditional authoring remains consumer-triggered depth.

### P2 — Adaptive Spatial Composition

**Creator journey.** Compose a curved gallery with paintings, plinths, tables, sculptures and lights.
Drag toward eligible surfaces without first selecting the surface. See contact, orientation and
alignment before acceptance. Move freely, use deliberate offsets or disable snapping; retain gizmos
and numeric precision. Compare arrangement directly in 3D with Unroll and return to check sightlines.
Use **Light this** on an object or region, then refine the ordinary Scene lighting proposal.

Now change the supporting geometry substantially: curve or shorten a Wall, split it, change a table
height, remove a support. The central question is whether revision preserves understandable authorship.

**Compare relationship promises, not just snap styles.**

| Approach | Honest promise | Discriminating consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pose-only | Placement accepts an ordinary world-local pose | Later geometry does not move it; no implied following |
| Provenance with explicit repair | Records the declared origin/relationship information without automatic rebasing | Can explain/repair a broken reference; cannot claim automatic recomputation |
| Bounded live attachment | Attempts to maintain an explicitly declared supported relationship | Needs owner geometry, correspondence, validity and fallback rules, not merely a cached transform |

The owner prefers best-effort preservation when an attachment is declared. A rigid painting stays
rigid on a curving Wall; an imperfect fit can remain visible for adjustment. If the support cannot be
resolved safely, retain a valid fallback pose where possible and report repair. A fallback is labeled
as such, never a competing authoritative placement. Never silently attach to an unrelated surface.

The comparison establishes which relationships deserve this behavior and how to expose it without
burdening every drag. It does **not** ratify live-attachment storage, evaluated versus rewritten
placement, or a general constraint solver. Scene object surfaces and Layout surfaces should share a
legible interaction while retaining their own geometry/identity authorities.

**Revision and failure cases.** Reverse a Wall, split at the attachment point, delete and recreate a
similarly named Wall, approach an Opening or finite edge, Undo/Redo, and reload after intervening
edits. Include both Wall sides, multiple artworks, intentional floating composition, and a rigid
painting whose corners no longer meet a curved surface. Compare a formal row with an intentionally
irregular arrangement. Check Light this from another viewpoint rather than judging a flattering
single image.

**Owner decision.** Is ordinary manipulation easier, with precision still accessible? Which following
behavior feels predictable? Is a visible imperfect fit preferable to an intrusive automatic
correction? Can the creator understand and repair an unresolved placement?

**Next boundary.** Bounded contact and pose operations can be planned independently. Persisted
provenance or following needs RR-4 and the attachment proof. The earlier E2 geometry spike supports
feasibility questions only; it did not establish persistent identity, reload continuity or product
value. Light this produces editable Scene lighting, not a universal inverse solver.

### P3 — Vertical Pavilion

**Creator journey.** Design a pavilion with split elevations, a mezzanine, atrium, skybridge or
comparable vertical relationship, and an expressive overhead treatment. Compose compression and
release: a low approach, a high interior, a lightwell and a sightline toward a focal object or empty
vista. Revise the structure and explain it through a visitor-facing Presentation using selected
Section, Reveal or Exploded representation.

Build a comparable experience around externally modeled architecture. Imported geometry need not
pretend to have native Wall/roof edit semantics. The comparison asks which native operations make
revision or explanation meaningfully better, and where an external modeling tool remains preferable.

**Explore rival spatial grammars on this same task.**

- Level/platform references with direct elevation handles versus named horizons and selective
  continuous Section. Test overlapping spaces and wings with incompatible heights; avoid assuming
  that the whole project shares one floor stack.
- Meaningful spatial-volume gestures versus Wall/profile-led authoring. A volume gesture can be a UX
  proposal without making Room blocks the canonical architecture or requiring automatic CSG.
- Ridge/eave controls versus sparse ribs and sectional profiles for a folded or curved canopy.
  Include forms extending beyond a Room footprint and a canopy with no enclosing Walls.
- Separate interior ceiling and exterior overhead expression where valuable: a calm interior below
  an expressive roof, variable ceiling elevations, lightwells and supported skylight gestures.
  Section, ghosting or isolation must make the actual edited surface clear.

Do not narrow exploration to flat caps/presets or existing operations merely because they are easy to
simulate. Equally, do not implement every study's geometry philosophy. Select the smallest native
family that delivers the demonstrated expressive value.

**Revision and failure cases.** Raise one mezzanine without moving another wing; change a roof profile
above an interior ceiling; edit near an acute ridge; move content across a Section plane without
losing the target; remove a supporting span; revise a canopy beyond its original footprint. Preserve
a usable path back from temporary inspection. Show how the visitor representation changes without
rewriting architecture or leaving authoring clipping active in Preview.

**Owner decision.** Which gestures make nontrivial form understandable and revisable? Which
representation solves occlusion? Does the ceiling offer value distinct from the exterior roof? Does
native modeling earn its engineering cost against import, particularly when the project changes?

**Next boundary.** RR-1 and RR-2 address only selected vertical/overhead consumers. A geometry proof
must extend the single canonical compiler, with explicit ownership still to be resolved where open.
Visitor representation invokes Layout-owned evaluation and Camera-owned viewing through Experience;
it is not a second architectural reconstruction or Camera evaluator. Full P3 depth is not a
prerequisite for useful near-term World/Experience work.

### P4 — Agent Authoring Harness

**Deferred by the owner, 2026-10-21.** The original proof brief below is retained for future use;
it is not commissioned or a prerequisite for convergence. Human/agent parity remains an invariant.

**Proof question.** Can a user's chosen supported LLM operate Biskiq as a controlled creative engine,
leaving ordinary human-editable state? A chat interface or conversational animation proves little.
The client must manipulate project meaning through canonical operations.

**Early bounded proof.** Use a small local TypeScript adapter where sufficient. Inspect the accepted
snapshot, canonical identities, supported operation kinds and required inputs. Propose a useful edit,
apply it through the existing registry/coordinator against the expected accepted revision, inspect
the result, Undo/Redo, and refine it after an unrelated human edit. Keep local revision identity
distinct from cloud Save's saved-base token.

The [engineering source/gap map](./engineering-authorization-roadmap.md#5-agent-proof-real-operations-and-exact-gaps)
distinguishes current Scene operations from provider-tested F2 View/Presentation operations.
Initially these may require separate admitted fixtures. That proves the specified operations, not a
unified live-editor workflow. Do not join them through raw JSON replacement or an agent-only Scene.

**Creative expansion.** As real supported operations arrive, use P1's watch and P2's gallery:
“Create an interactive explanation using the actual supported demonstrations, close-up Views and
contextual text; revise the framing, change a control, and reuse the Presentation elsewhere.”
Report unavailable control, clip, reuse or attachment operations as exact gaps. Do not implement a
speculative general platform merely to make this whole sentence appear successful.

**Discriminating cases.**

- Request a nonexistent clip, an unsupported operation and an invalid reference; receive precise
  diagnostics with no accepted partial change.
- Prepare an edit, accept a human change, then apply the stale proposal. Refuse, re-read and propose
  a narrow revision preserving unrelated accepted work; no silent auto-rebase.
- Fail part of a compound Camera/Presentation operation; no orphan View or half-installed composition.
  Undo/Redo preserves captured identities while revision identity remains fresh.
- Inspect or Preview through supported mechanisms, then manually refine the accepted state. Label
  inspection-only evidence honestly if the relevant Preview is not yet connected.

If a genuine external/local model connection is feasible within the authorized prototype scope,
test it and record the model, transport, operation trace and outcome. Otherwise separate deterministic
semantic-operation evidence from simulated LLM orchestration. A deterministic script is not proof
that an LLM can plan the task.

**Decision.** Technical evidence establishes valid operations and failures; owner judgment establishes
whether the assisted creative workflow is useful and controllable. Missing semantic discovery can
justify a small adapter. Production MCP/REST interfaces, authentication, provider integration,
collaboration and general agent frameworks require separate scope; none is a prerequisite here.

## 3. Runnable quality and review packet

Each creative prototype must be developed enough for serious judgment: realistic content, usable
authoring interactions, direct Preview, revision and clear failure/repair behavior where relevant.
A scripted presenter can help explain the experience, but cannot replace usable authoring. Polish only
until the creative question and tradeoffs are visible.

Provide a concise packet with:

- The creative problem, competing approaches and a strong ordinary/simpler comparator.
- How to open and exercise it, with reproducible starting/revision states where useful.
- What is **real**, **prepared**, **simulated** or **unsupported**, including source revision and runner
  for any reused production operation.
- The owner's decision, unresolved technical claims and the consequence of adoption or rejection.

Use realistic fixtures without turning them into an asset-production project. A prepared clip is
acceptable source material; it must not masquerade as an animation authored by Biskiq. A flat image/text
comparison can demonstrate that no spatial treatment is needed. Preserve keyboard access, readable
content, narrow layouts and reduced-motion alternatives in the relevant journeys; do not invent
numerical UX gates.

## 4. Verdict and synthesis

The owner can adopt, adopt with named changes, request iteration, reject or park a direction, including
separate verdicts for different aspects. Record the reviewed revision, actual verdict, required changes
and consequences in the prototype's own evidence. Review agents and automated tests cannot fill in an
owner verdict. No fixed number of iterations applies.

A good repair treatment may be adopted while its live-following mechanism remains unproved. A useful
instrument may be accepted as UX while its PLATE departure awaits ratification. Neither case authorizes
production. Update the coordination ledger when a recorded decision changes a disposition; keep a
rejected direction's evidence so it is not repeatedly rediscovered.

Return to the [convergence route](./README.md#3-boundary-test-and-convergence). Do not require a new
research round or second full prototype if accepted contracts and evidence already settle the next
consumer. Do not carry mock formats, snapshot Undo or prototype evaluators into production.

## 5. Technical proofs

These are targeted evidence inside or alongside the relevant prototype, not another portfolio or
mandatory preliminary program.

| Proof | Concrete claim and adversarial evidence | Opens for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset truth | Inspect actual files: flat/nested parts, repeated names, pivots, supplied clips, two instances, changed units/transforms, re-export reorder/removal. Distinguish a file feature, admitted engine capability and durable reference guarantee | P1's truthful fixture; P4 addressing; RR-5 only when durable semantics are needed |
| Invocation/reset | Run identity, cancel/skip/exit, re-entry before late completion, repeated invocation, same-channel conflict and an unrelated active contribution. Canonical source stays unchanged; cleanup cannot reset another run | P1's supported invocation gap, RR-3 |
| Attachment continuity | Exact owner geometry/face orientation, finite edges/Openings, split/reversal/delete/recreate, Undo/Redo, non-adjacent revision and reload. Explicit fallback/repair; never infer correspondence by rebuilding Layout in Scene | P2's persisted relationship candidate, RR-4 |
| Bounded overhead geometry | Selected profiles/planes/ribs against canonical compiler validity: acute/degenerate edges, Wall-top agreement, openings/lightwells where supported, revision and refusal. State which shapes remain unsupported | Accepted P3 family, RR-2 |
| Real-stack content fidelity | Projection, occlusion, hit testing, readable semantic equivalent and input access at actual Camera states; distinguish prepared HTML labels from supported content semantics | A P1/P3 claim that depends on the real rendering/input stack |

Technical failures can limit a claim or require a different approach. They do not judge taste, and a
positive owner verdict does not establish technical feasibility. Use small adversarial fixtures
sufficient to expose the risk, not a speculative benchmark platform.

## 6. Protected production boundaries

Exploratory UI can differ radically; semantic claims must remain honest. Keep Layout topology and
representations under one compiler, Scene placement world-local with explicit internal frames, Camera
evaluation under one authority, and Experience editorial order separate from reusable performances.
No invisible Stops, duplicate writable Presentation order, silent identity retargeting, inferred
attachment from overlap, or runtime writes into authored truth.

The [prototype family boundary](../../prototypes/README.md) permits disposable simulations and reused
pinned modules, never production importing prototypes. A mock evaluator is not evidence that a new
production evaluator is allowed. Real cross-domain claims require one expected revision, atomic
acceptance and ordinary history. Svelte 5 runes/Threlte apply where actual consuming-stack behavior is
being tested, not as a restriction on every disposable sketch.

A local host-control demonstration can explore invocation without publishing anything. R1-D still
freezes T4 planning and implementation: no production embed, prepared reader, release or share-route
work follows from a prototype verdict.
