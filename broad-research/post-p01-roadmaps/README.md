# Two tracks toward a more capable Biskiq

Biskiq is a visual and AI-addressable creative environment for authoring spatial worlds and directing
interactive 3D Experiences. A project can begin with a watch in an empty World, imported architecture,
native Walls or an installation. Rooms and Guides are optional. Its value is making content
composable, interactive, revisable and reusable, through visual and semantic authoring.

This is the single coordination record for the
[engineering roadmap](./engineering-authorization-roadmap.md) and
[prototype program](./prototype-roadmap.md). The directory's historical “post-P01” name is not a
requirement to finish all foundations before exploring design. The tracks run concurrently and meet
at a concrete production consumer.

## 1. Recommendation and independent assessment

Preserve the prior proposal's strongest distinction: engineering can progress on justified foundations
while prototypes discover unsettled creative interactions. Replace its copied P01 status/authorization
schedule and fragmented X1–X18 dependency graph. Neither was a durable program.

The revised program has **three creative experiences and one shared-engine proof**:

- **P1 Interactive Presentation Studio:** turn a capability-bearing imported object into an interactive
  explanation; design controls, bind supported actions, Preview, revise and reuse it in an exhibition.
- **P2 Adaptive Spatial Composition:** arrange a curved gallery and substantially revise its supporting
  geometry without losing authorship; compare intelligent placement and explicit attachment policies.
- **P3 Vertical Pavilion:** explore expressive elevation, roof/ceiling and representation gestures,
  judged against the option of importing architecture.
- **P4 Agent Authoring Harness:** prove that an authoring client can inspect, propose, apply and refine
  canonical edits. It joins P1/P2 as real operations become available; it is not a fourth simulated UX
  flagship or a prerequisite for their exploration.

This retains the brief's four concerns but changes their dependency structure. P1 is the first
substantial creative deliverable. P2 and P3 need no prior prototype verdict to explore. P4 starts with
a useful supported subset and states the remaining interface gaps; it must not claim the full watch
workflow from a scripted facade. Composition and revision are tested inside each journey, not saved
for a final integration experiment.

Conservatory remains the strong accepted design reference. Requiring every experiment to inherit its
Card/Stage arrangement would preselect the answer. Explore different surfaces first; synthesize the
owner-selected ideas into Biskiq afterward. Conversely, “engineering never waits” is too broad:
authorized foundations continue, but design-dependent formats stop at their actual unresolved boundary.

## 3. Boundary test and convergence

**Track A — engineering foundations:** continue within existing authorizations and accepted contracts.
A desirable capability, a proposed milestone criterion or a BUILD disposition is not permission to
start a new slice.

**Track B — creative exploration:** test demanding end-to-end tasks with divergent editing surfaces.
Only the owner accepts or rejects product UX. Tests establish technical claims; reviewers identify
risks and recommend decisions. Neither substitutes for that verdict.

For each proposed engineering increment, ask:

1. **Justification:** which ratified promise, owner ruling, concrete defect or accepted ordinary
   workflow requires it?
2. **Consumer:** which actual workflow exercises it, and which track owns that work?
3. **Open choice:** would choosing its persistence, identity, lifecycle or interaction now pre-empt a
   material unresolved design decision?
4. **Boundary:** what useful bounded operation can ship without that commitment, and what exact
   question prevents the remainder?

Build the common useful part when a consumer and authorization exist. Do not build a general mechanism
merely because several imagined directions might use it. Where accepted evidence already settles the
question, neither a new prototype nor a research round is compulsory.

The convergence route is:

**Owner UX verdict → focused technical research if needed → design/contract reconciliation if needed
→ register and authorize the consuming production work → implement, verify, review and close out.**

Asset inspection may establish honest starting material before UX review; it does not require a
full semantic-ingest research program. P4 and narrow probes test technical claims directly. A
convincing mockup cannot prove a compiler, runtime lifecycle or persistence model. If feasibility
contradicts an adopted UX, report the precise failed claim and return that part for revision; neither
silently downgrade the accepted behavior nor force the architecture to imitate the mock.

**DEFER** has no present production consumer. Mixed dispositions apply to different depths of a

## 5. Focused research triggers

These are a question index, not seven mandatory projects or a sequence. Combine questions when one
consumer needs them; close with existing evidence if it suffices. Each opened inquiry produces a small
decision packet: problem, inspected evidence, competing models, recommendation, rejected alternatives,
first consumer, and any amendment needed. Record the exact absent interface where proof is blocked.

| Concern | Opens when | Evidence needed to decide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RR-1 · Levels and vertical frames | P3 selects a viable native direction, or T1 prepares its first level consumer | Irregular/overlapping heights, structure frames, identity, Plan/Section context and revision semantics; no inferred Room ownership |
| RR-2 · Roof/ceiling ownership and geometry | P3 identifies a worthwhile overhead family and implementation is considered | Interior/exterior responsibility, Wall-top agreement, invalid edits and bounded compiler extension; compare importing |
| RR-3 · Invocation, cancellation and coordinated execution | P1/P3's accepted invocation needs a contract or evaluator capability not already available | Domain channel ownership, run identity, baseline/reset scope, concurrent contributions and stale callbacks; independent clocks only if needed |
| RR-4 · Persistent relationships and correspondence | P2's provenance or preferred following becomes a production candidate | Exact owner geometry, explicit reference semantics, split/reversal/delete, identity non-reuse, Undo/reload, fallback and repair |
| RR-5 · Semantic asset components and reuse | Real-file evidence or P1/P4 requires durable component/role addressing | Actual clips/pivots, repeated instances/names, re-export correspondence, incompatible binding and resource revisions |
| RR-6 · Experience UI/content representation | P1 selects a grammar that admitted formats cannot express | Visitor UI versus Scene subjects, accessible events/targets/actions, projected labels/media, ordinary editing and reuse; no website model |
| RR-7 · Advanced conditions and visitor state | A real Gates/branching consumer needs operators beyond supported ones | Declared typed state, deterministic evaluation, explicit initial state and missing-binding diagnostics; no implicit visit memory |

The [technical proofs](./prototype-roadmap.md#5-technical-proofs) are inputs to these decisions, not
additional acceptance rounds imposed on every prototype.

## 6. Contract and authorization routes

| Change discovered | Required route before production |
| --- | --- |
| A novel editing surface fits existing semantics and PLATE | Synthesize it into the consuming design; ordinary slice authorization |
| An accepted surface changes a PLATE rule | Name the exact departure and alternatives; propose an owner-ratified amendment through PLATE's conformance process |
| New identity, persisted unit, channel/operator or compound intent | Domain-owned first-consumer proposal through F's extension/amendment procedure and gate register where applicable |
| New Layout family, attachment policy or reusable resource | Resolve domain ownership and validity first; amend the relevant contract only if needed |
| Changed phase scope or transferred obligation | Owner-authorized bounded replanning, track/phase registration and the applicable runbook |
| Changed stability criterion or delivery eligibility | Separate milestone owner decision; neither prototype acceptance nor this roadmap changes R1-D |

Use [F extension points](../../docs/reference/composition-execution.md#extension-points-and-version-negotiation),
[PLATE conformance](../../docs/reference/design-system/editor-shell-and-visual-system.md#conformance-test-for-future-design),
the [gate register](../../docs/roadmap/README.md#gate-register) and
[bounded replanning](../../docs/operations/README.md#bounded-replanning). No speculative amendment
catalogue is required before a first consumer exposes a real gap.

The owner decisions remaining here are bounded: accept/revise this program as planning direction;
authorize a concrete prototype/proof scope; judge its demonstrated UX; and later authorize the named
production consumer and any necessary contract change. These are different decisions, not compulsory
repeat approvals for ordinary reversible work within an already authorized scope.
